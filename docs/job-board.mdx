# Job board

```tsx
import { JobBoard, JobCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom/job-board';

<JobBoard
  jobs={jobs}
  payBands={payBands}
  onTake={take}
  onPass={hide}
  onRefresh={pull}
/>
```

`JobCard` is one piece of work. `JobBoard` is the work on offer, filtered, ordered and counted.

## It is the offer card seen from the other side

`carrier-quote`'s `CarrierQuoteCard` is a carrier saying what they would charge to move something. This is the same object read by the person who would move it: the same blocks in the same order, the same tile row, the same 420 threshold at which the actions drop their labels, the same footer over a hairline. Both were built from the same measurements on purpose — a courier app shows one of these on the board and the other in the customer's app, and two different geometries would make them look like two products.

What differs is only whose number the headline is, and what stands where the person does:

| | `CarrierQuoteCard` | `JobCard` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the mark | the carrier's 48 avatar | the 48 vehicle tile |
| the identity | the carrier's name and rating | what is being moved |
| the headline | the price asked | the pay offered |
| the middle | three tiles | the route, then three tiles |
| the footer | decline · message · accept | pass · take |

## The geometry

- **Card.** Outlined, radius 20, 16 inset, 16 between blocks. Chosen, it takes a 2px border and gives the pixel back out of its own inset, so nothing inside moves.
- **Mark.** 48 comfortable, 36 compact, radius 12, at the surface's next fill up. It carries the glyph for `vehicleKind`, or `vehicleIcon` when the app names one. With neither it is the route glyph — a marketplace that routes five bodies has five glyphs, and guessing between them is worse than not drawing one.
- **Tiles.** Distance, time, window. One row over 420, two columns under it, an odd tile keeping its half.
- **Compact.** 64 tall, the mark, the load, one meta line, the pay. No route, no tiles, no breakdown: that density is a LIST ROW, and a list row that drew a route would be a card with the padding taken out.

## The route is `RouteStops`, not a second pin column

`pickup`, `via` and `dropoff` become that family's stops, at its `compact` density. It already owns the marker shapes, the connector that fills behind a journey, the position words and the announced name; a second column drawn here would be a fourth marker vocabulary in one library.

## What a job is CLOSED does not remove it

`state` is `open`, `taken` or `expired`. A closed job keeps its place, dims to the disabled opacity, and says which of the two happened — as a `Badge` beside the load and again where the take action was. The take action is GONE rather than greyed out: a dimmed primary button invites a press that can never succeed.

This is the one behaviour the board exists to get right. A row that vanishes under a thumb is indistinguishable from a mis-tap, and the reader is left unsure whether they took the job or lost it. `filterJobOffers` answers the reader's four questions and nothing else, so nothing about a job's state can make it disappear.

## The filters

Four independent questions, combined with AND, each a sideways-scrolling `ChipRow`:

| | reads | default bands |
| --- | --- | --- |
| distance | `distanceKm` | `JOB_DISTANCE_BANDS` — any, 3, 10, 25 km |
| pay | `payValue` | **none** |
| when | `startsInMinutes` | `JOB_WHEN_BANDS` — any, hour, 4 hours, today |
| vehicle | `vehicleKind` | the five `vehicle-picker` knows |

**`payBands` has no default and will not get one.** A band is words plus an amount, and the amount is a currency. A kilometre and a minute are nobody's; twenty euros is somebody's.

**A filter cannot exclude what it cannot measure.** A job with no `distanceKm` survives every distance band; one with no `payValue` survives every pay floor. Treating a missing value as zero is how a filter hides work from a courier because the app omitted a field.

**Vehicle is a set of toggles, not a radiogroup.** A courier with a van can take a bike job, and "van or box truck" is a sentence the board has to be able to say. Pressing the only selected vehicle clears the dimension.

## The order

`sortJobOffers` is stable, and PAY sorts high first while distance, start and expiry sort low first — a courier scanning for money wants the biggest number and scanning for distance wants the smallest, so one direction would be wrong three times. A job with no number for the current key goes LAST, keeping the caller's order inside that group: there is nothing left to sort it on, and re-shuffling a group of equals makes a board jump for no visible reason.

## Traps

- **Money is never parsed.** `pay`, `payNote` and every `payLines` amount are drawn exactly as given. `payValue` is a separate number for ordering and the pay filter, and it is never drawn. Nothing here adds, converts or rounds.
- **The card is not one big button.** `onPressJob` is bound to the load block; the actions sit outside it. A control inside a control is invalid on web and renders fine either way.
- **The actions lose their labels, never their names.** Under 420 each drops to a glyph and keeps `"<action> <load>"`.
- **`disabled` is a constraint, not a default.** It combines with the job's own state, so a caller cannot re-enable a job the marketplace has already given away.
- **The refresh control does not spin.** Bloom draws no press animations; while `refreshing` it is inert, because a second pull would race the first.
- **The empty state offers the undo it caused.** With a filter narrowed and no `emptyAction`, the clear control is drawn there.
