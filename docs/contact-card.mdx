# ContactProfileCard

One component, two densities, two subjects.

```tsx
import { ContactProfileCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom';

<ContactProfileCard
  name="Nora Vance"
  role="Head of Operations"
  company="Larkspur Freight"
  status={{ label: 'Customer', tone: 'success' }}
  channels={[
    { kind: 'email', onPress: composeEmail },
    { kind: 'phone', onPress: dial },
  ]}
  owner={{ name: 'Marta Oyeleye' }}
  tags={['Enterprise', 'Renewal Q4']}
  lastTouch="Last contacted 6 days ago"
  onPress={openRecord}
/>
```

## Why it is not called `ContactCard`

`listing-details` already publishes a `ContactCard`: the card a renter uses to
reach the person LETTING a home. Two families cannot offer one name from two
declarations — ES modules drop the name from the barrel outright when two star
exports disagree about it, and bob's CommonJS output resolves it first-wins
instead, so the two builds of one file would disagree about what `ContactCard`
is (gate: `barrel-name-collisions.test.ts`).

The two are genuinely different things — an ACTION card for reaching a listing's
agent, and the RECORD of a customer — so this one is the contact's profile and
says so. The family directory stays `contact-card`.

## A company is the same card

`kind="company"` does not switch components. It squares the avatar into a
squircle so a logo reads as a logo, reads `role` as the INDUSTRY, and drops
`company` — a company has no company. Everything else is the same slot with a
different fact in it: `facts` carries the headcount and the sites, `people`
carries the facepile of who works there, and the owner, the tags and the last
touch mean exactly what they meant for a person.

That is the whole reason it is a `kind` and not a `CompanyCard`. Two components
would be the same card plus a fork, and the fork would drift — one of them would
get the owner row, the other would get the status pill, and nothing would say
which was intended.

## The geometry

It is built to the four blocks every Bloom record card is built to —
`home-search`'s `SavedSearchCard` is the reference, and the `BesideTheReference`
story puts the two in one shot:

| block | what it holds |
| --- | --- |
| header | a 44 leading mark, the name at `body-semibold` (two lines), a status `Badge` opposite |
| meta | ONE secondary line (the role and the account), then the last touch, the facts and the tags as OUTLINED chips, wrapped 6 apart |
| facepile | the people at a company, when there are any |
| footer | a hairline, the quiet owner label on the left, the channel controls on the right |

| | comfortable | compact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| surface | a `Card` (`outlined`, `radius-16`, 16 / 12 / 16 / 16) | none — the list owns it |
| mark | 44 | 36 |
| name | `body-semibold`, 2 lines | `body-semibold`, 1 line |
| chips, facepile, owner | drawn | not drawn |
| last touch | a chip in the meta row | a quiet third line |
| channels | in the footer | beside the identity |
| height | what it needs | at least 64 |

Compact is the ROW, not a small card. It deliberately draws no surface of its
own: a list, a settings group or a panel is already painting one, and a row with
a second fill inside it is the seam every list of cards ends up with. It also
drops the tags, the facts, the facepile and the owner rather than shrinking
them — a row that showed all of them would be a card with the padding taken
out, which is not a different density, it is a worse card.

### Where the status pill sits, and what gives way

On a CARD the status pill sits beside the name. In a ROW it moves to the meta
line, because the row does not have the width: at 390, three 44-wide channel
actions and a 36 avatar leave the identity about 150px, and a pill beside the
name there truncated both — measured as "Nora Van…" next to "Custo…". The pill
itself never shrinks (it is one word, and half of it says nothing); the line
beside it is what ellipsizes.

That 150px is also the budget the `lastTouch` line is spending. A row with three
channels at 390 will clip it to "Last contacted 6 days…". If that line matters
more than the third action, pass two channels and put the rest behind
`actions`.

## The press target is the identity block, not the card

`onPress` is bound to the avatar and the text, never to the whole surface, and
the channel actions and the `actions` slot sit outside it.

A pressable card wrapping pressable channels is a control inside a control:
invalid on web (a `<button>` inside a `<button>`), ambiguous on native, and it
RENDERS FINE either way — the browser does not complain in a way anyone reads,
and a prop-level test sees two correct components. `ContactProfileCard.test.tsx`
asserts the containment instead: the subject element contains no element with
`role="button"`, and no button in the tree has a button ancestor.

The identity block is still the largest target on either density, so scoping it
costs nothing.

## A channel is a CONTROL, not a glyph

`channels` never renders an address or a number as text, and it never renders a
bare icon either. Each entry is a `Button` — `variant="secondary" size="small"
iconOnly` — the same control the reference card puts in its footer, so it
carries the surface, the border, the hover, the disabled treatment and the
focus ring every other Bloom action has.

Bare icons floating on a card surface are not an affordance anywhere else in
this library. They shipped here once and were the single biggest reason the card
did not read as Bloom: nothing said the glyph could be pressed, and nothing gave
a finger anything to hit. `ContactProfileCard.test.tsx` asserts the emitted box —
a `<button>` with a fill, a border and a height — because a glyph and a control
both render.

The control DRAWS at 32, the size a card wants, and is HIT at 44 through
`CONTACT_CHANNEL_HIT`, the size a thumb needs. A contact list is a column of
these, so both numbers have to be true.

Each is named `"{verb} {label ?? name}"` from `CONTACT_CHANNEL` — "Email Nora
Vance", "Call Nora Vance", "Open the website of Quillon Health". The verb lives
in the table rather than on the entry for two reasons: an icon-only control
draws no text, so ARIA computes no name for it and one has to be built; and
building it here means a caller cannot ship an unnamed one by forgetting a prop.

Pass `icon` on a channel to change the glyph; the KIND still decides the verb,
so a custom glyph cannot silently change what the action announces.

## The meta row is one row of OUTLINED chips

The last touch, the `facts` and the `tags` are all short facts about the same
subject, so they are one wrapped row of `Chip size="medium" variant="outlined"` —
the reference's criteria row. Three stacked quiet lines is what the card had
before, and it is why nothing on it read as the subject.

**Outlined, not filled.** A neutral FILLED pill reads as a disabled control:
Bloom's filled chips are the selected state, and a card full of them says every
fact has been switched off. Outlined at the `default` tone takes
`colors.textSecondary` for both the label and the border, which is the pair the
palette already ships for a quiet token.

`lastTouchTone` is the one exception: with a tone the last-touch chip turns
`subtle` in that tone, because "41 days ago" IS a state and the card is being
asked to say so.

This card never edits a tag: the INPUT is `tag-field`, a family of its own.

## The leading mark is neutral

`Avatar` derives a pastel tint from the name unless it is told otherwise, which
is right in a people LIST — the colour is what tells two rows apart — and wrong
on a record card, where the subject is already named and the tint reads as a
status nobody set. So the mark is `color="neutral"`, the quiet disc, at 44 to
match the reference's icon tile.

The facepile under a company keeps its tints (`showInitials`): four colleagues
with no photo would otherwise be four identical grey discs, which is the case
the tint exists for.

## Colour

Every colour is read off the fill the content actually lands on, through
`styles/surface-levels.ts`: the card resolves against `theme.colors.card`, the
compact row against whatever `useSurfaceFill()` reports. `lastTouch` is the
quiet chip by default; with `lastTouchTone` it takes that tone's accent PAIR
through `resolveAccentColors` — never the fill colour with an alpha appended to
it, which is the failure `docs/badge.mdx` describes.

The comfortable card publishes its own fill with `SurfaceLevelProvider`, so
anything nested inside it (a meter, a second row, a menu) steps off the card
rather than off the page.
