# ContactProfileCard

One component, two densities, two subjects.

```tsx
import { ContactProfileCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom';

<ContactProfileCard
  name="Nora Vance"
  role="Head of Operations"
  company="Larkspur Freight"
  status={{ label: 'Customer', tone: 'success' }}
  channels={[
    { kind: 'email', onPress: composeEmail },
    { kind: 'phone', onPress: dial },
  ]}
  headline={{ label: 'Open pipeline', value: '€248,000', delta: '+2 deals' }}
  stats={[
    { value: '4', label: 'Open deals' },
    { value: '€62k', label: 'Avg deal' },
    { value: '6 days', label: 'Last touch' },
    { value: '3h', label: 'Replies in' },
  ]}
  owner={{ name: 'Marta Oyeleye' }}
  tags={['Enterprise', 'Renewal Q4']}
  lastTouch="Last contacted 6 days ago"
  onPress={openRecord}
/>
```

## Why it is not called `ContactCard`

`listing-details` already publishes a `ContactCard`: the card a renter uses to
reach the person LETTING a home. Two families cannot offer one name from two
declarations — ES modules drop the name from the barrel outright when two star
exports disagree about it, and bob's CommonJS output resolves it first-wins
instead, so the two builds of one file would disagree about what `ContactCard`
is (gate: `barrel-name-collisions.test.ts`).

The two are genuinely different things — an ACTION card for reaching a listing's
agent, and the RECORD of a customer — so this one is the contact's profile and
says so. The family directory stays `contact-card`.

## A company is the same card

`kind="company"` does not switch components. It squares the avatar into a
squircle so a logo reads as a logo, reads `role` as the INDUSTRY, and drops
`company` — a company has no company. Everything else is the same slot with a
different fact in it: `stats` carries the headcount and the deal count, `people`
carries the facepile of who works there, and the owner, the tags and the last
touch mean exactly what they meant for a person.

That is the whole reason it is a `kind` and not a `CompanyCard`. Two components
would be the same card plus a fork, and the fork would drift.

## The geometry

It is drawn the way Bloom draws a RECORD — `ai-profile-card` is the register,
and the `BesideTheReference` story puts the two in one shot at the same width:

| block | what it holds |
| --- | --- |
| cover | a band across the top of the card: the caller's image, or an accent wash in `coverTone` |
| mark | the 72 avatar, hanging half off the band's bottom edge, with the channel actions opposite it |
| identity | the name at `title-2-medium`, then ONE line — the role and the account — with the status `Badge` beside it |
| figure | a quiet `body-medium` label over the `headline` number at `title-1-medium`, with a tinted delta `Chip` |
| tiles | one `stats` tile per number: the value over what it counts |
| tags | ONE scrolling `ChipRow` — the last touch, the `facts`, the `tags` |
| footer | the facepile, and the owner as a quiet attribution line |

| | comfortable | compact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| surface | a `Card` (`outlined`, `radius-20`) | none — the list owns it |
| cover | 96 tall | none |
| mark | 72, overlapping the cover | 36, inside the press target |
| name | `title-2-medium`, 2 lines | `body-semibold`, 1 line |
| figure, tiles, chips, facepile, owner | drawn | not drawn |
| last touch | the first chip in the row | a quiet third line |
| channels | labelled, beside the mark | glyphs, beside the identity |
| height | what it needs | at least 64 |

Compact is the ROW, not a small card. It deliberately draws no surface and no
cover: a list, a settings group or a panel is already painting one, and a row
with a band across it is a card with the padding taken out — which is not a
different density, it is a worse card.

### There are no negative margins in the overlap

The cover is absolutely positioned across the top of the card and the content
block simply starts `CONTACT_COVER_HEIGHT - CONTACT_AVATAR_OVERLAP` down. The
mark is therefore the first thing in normal flow, it overlaps the band by half
its own height, and everything under it stacks without an offset. Pulling the
mark up with a margin would have been one number that every later block had to
know about.

## The card measures ITSELF

`CONTACT_NARROW_WIDTH` (480) is compared against the card's OWN laid-out width,
through `useContainerWidth` on the content block — not against the window. The
same card is 358 wide in a phone column and 900 wide in a record pane, and the
window cannot tell those apart.

Under it, two things change together: the channel actions drop their labels to
glyphs, and the tiles stack two by two. They are one threshold rather than two
because a card too narrow for four tiles is too narrow for three labelled
buttons beside a 72 mark.

Before the first layout the width is unknown and the labels are ON
(`contactActionsAreLabelled(null)`). That is the better of the two guesses: an
unmeasured card is one a caller has not constrained.

## A channel is a labelled CONTROL

Each entry in `channels` is a `Button` — `variant="secondary" size="small"` with
the kind's glyph and its ACTION word ("Email", "Call", "Meet") — so it carries
the surface, the border, the hover, the disabled treatment and the focus ring
every other Bloom action has. Bare icons floating on a card surface are not an
affordance anywhere else in this library.

**The label and the NAME are different things.** The label is dropped on a
narrow card; the name never is. Every control is named `"{verb} {label ?? name}"`
from `CONTACT_CHANNEL` — "Email Nora Vance", "Open the website of Quillon
Health" — so an `iconOnly` channel is still named, and a caller cannot ship an
unnamed one by forgetting a prop. Pass `icon` on a channel to change the glyph;
the KIND still decides the verb and the word.

The control DRAWS at 32, the size a card wants, and is HIT at 44 through
`CONTACT_CHANNEL_HIT`, the size a thumb needs.

## The press target is the identity block, not the card

`onPress` is bound to the text (and, in a row, the mark with it), never to the
whole surface; the channels and the `actions` slot sit outside it.

A pressable card wrapping pressable channels is a control inside a control:
invalid on web, ambiguous on native, and it RENDERS FINE either way — a
prop-level test sees two correct components. `ContactProfileCard.test.tsx`
asserts the containment instead.

## The numbers come before the labels

`headline` and `stats` are the shape of the card; the chips are what is left
over. That order is the claim the card makes, and the suite asserts it by
document position rather than by looking at the styles — a record is read for
its figures.

A tile is the value over what it counts, on the next fill UP from the card
(`surfaceFillOn`), with its own text rungs read off that tile — `neutral-100` is
right on white and invisible on a dark card, which is why nothing here names a
ramp stop.

**`stat-cards` could not express this tile.** `StatCard` is a 132-tall KPI card
with a mandatory icon tile and a mandatory delta chip, and `StatCards` picks its
column count from the WINDOW width, so four of them inside a 420-wide record
card is not a layout that exists. The tile here is `ai-profile-card`'s — value
over label, a rounded inset panel — on Bloom's radius scale rather than that
card's raw 10. If the two are ever unified, this is the tile to lift.

## The chips are ONE line

The last touch, the `facts` and the `tags` are one `ChipRow`, which SCROLLS
rather than wrapping. A wrapping wall of pills is what the tiles replaced; the
leftovers of a record take one line, or they take the card over.

`lastTouchTone` is the one exception to the outline: with a tone the last-touch
chip turns `subtle` in that tone, because "41 days ago" IS a state and the card
is being asked to say so. Everything else is `variant="outlined"` — a neutral
FILLED pill reads as a disabled control.

This card never edits a tag: the INPUT is `tag-field`, a family of its own.

## The leading mark is neutral

`Avatar` derives a pastel tint from the name unless it is told otherwise, which
is right in a people LIST — the colour is what tells two rows apart — and wrong
on a record card, where the subject is already named and the tint reads as a
status nobody set. So the mark is `color="neutral"`.

The facepile under a company keeps its tints (`showInitials`): four colleagues
with no photo would otherwise be four identical grey discs, which is the case
the tint exists for.

## Colour

Every colour is read off the fill the content actually lands on, through
`styles/surface-levels.ts`: the card resolves against `theme.colors.card`, the
compact row against whatever `useSurfaceFill()` reports, and a tile against the
step above whichever of those it is. The cover wash and the delta pill come from
`resolveAccentColors` — the tone's PAIR, never the fill colour with an alpha
appended to it, which is the failure `docs/badge.mdx` describes.

The comfortable card publishes its own fill with `SurfaceLevelProvider`, so
anything nested inside it steps off the card rather than off the page.
