# Place list

```tsx
import { PlaceList, PlaceListCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom/place-list';
```

"Want to go", "Favourites", "Coffee, ranked". `PlaceListCard` is the list seen from
outside; `PlaceList` is what is inside it.

## `PlaceListCard`

```tsx
<PlaceListCard
  name="Want to go"
  count={12}
  photos={[a, b, c, d]}
  icon={RiFlagLine}
  visibility="shared"
  collaborators={people}
  onPress={open}
/>
```

- **The cover is a STRIP, not a square.** 112 tall — `listing-card`'s compact thumbnail
  height, which is the height of the place rows the list is made of, so a list's cover and
  the first place under it agree. The first tile takes two shares of the row and the rest
  one each, with 2px seams.
- **The count and the sharing are ONE line.** A saved list is scanned in a grid of them,
  and the only questions asked of a card in that grid are "which list is this" and "how
  much is in it".
- **`color` is the person's, not a token.** A list's colour is a choice, and Bloom has no
  ramp for it. It paints the glyph and tints the empty strip; nothing derives anything from
  it.
- **The card is one press target, so it announces one sentence** — "Want to go, 12 places,
  Shared with 3, Shared". Left to ARIA's contents algorithm it is four runs with no commas.
- **`AvatarGroup` takes no `onPressItem` here.** A button inside the card's own press target
  is the nested control this library refuses everywhere else; a collaborator's profile
  belongs on the list's own screen.

It is deliberately NOT `listing-card`'s `WishlistCard`. That card is a square 2×2 mosaic
with its text underneath, built for a grid of wishlists; this one is a wide card with a body
that has to carry a count, three faces and a visibility badge. They share the idea and none
of the geometry, so sharing the component would have meant a second layout inside it.

## `PlaceList`

The places inside are `PlaceCard` at `density="row"`, untouched — a saved place and a search
result are the same object, so this list draws no row of its own and inherits the thumbnail,
the rating, the open state and the composed announcement with it.

**The note is a sibling of the place, not part of it.** The place is the place; the note is
what someone said about it. It is announced as "Note: get the sourdough before noon", after
the row.

### Reordering is buttons, and the buttons are the accessible path

`moveItem` (`hooks/list-reorder`) is the same list maths the track list, the queue and the
photo grid use, so "move" means one thing in this package.

There is no pointer drag. `SortablePhotoGrid`'s drag is GRID geometry — a point resolved to
a cell in a fixed row of columns — and a column of rows of different heights is a different
problem, not a smaller one. Buttons are also what a keyboard and a screen reader get out of
a drag surface anyway, so the list is fully operable with the half that exists. If you want
a swipe to delete on touch, wrap a row in [`SwipeRow`](/docs/swipe-row) — that is the app's
call, not this component's.

Two properties that are easy to lose:

- **Every row is keyed by its id**, so on web the button that was just pressed keeps focus
  and travels with its row. A reader can press "move later" three times without hunting for
  the control again.
- **The ends are DISABLED, not absent.** The first row's "move earlier" and the last row's
  "move later" stay in place; a control that disappears at the end of its travel moves the
  two beside it under the pointer.

Every move is announced politely through a visually hidden live region, because a reorder
draws nothing new — the rows just swap, and a screen reader would otherwise hear nothing at
all.

### Controls are 44

The move and remove controls are `GlyphButton`s at 44 with a 20 glyph, right-aligned under
the row rather than stacked beside it: three 44 boxes do not fit in the 112 a place row is
tall, and a column of controls narrower than that is not a thumb target.

## Geometry

`PLACE_LIST_GEOMETRY`: a 112 cover at radius 12 with 2px seams and at most four photos, 12
between blocks, 44 controls with a 20 glyph.

## Labels

Every word `PlaceList` speaks that is not in the data is in `PlaceListLabels`, with
`DEFAULT_PLACE_LIST_LABELS` as the English set. `PLACE_LIST_VISIBILITY_LABELS` and
`PLACE_LIST_VISIBILITY_ICON` carry the three visibility words and their glyphs.
