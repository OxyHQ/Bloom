# Earnings

```tsx
import { EarningsSummary } from '@oxy.so/bloom/earnings';

<EarningsSummary periods={[day, week, month]} defaultPeriod="week" formatAxisValue={euros} />
```

`EarningsSummary` is the whole panel. `EarningsBreakdown` and `EarningsPayoutRow` are two of its four parts, published because a screen often wants one of them alone.

## It is built AROUND the chart card, not beside it

`chart-cards`' `EarningsChartCard` is already four of the five things a courier's earnings screen needs: the period's figure, the change on the period before, the Day / Week / Month switch and the bars of the last weeks. Drawing a second figure and a second switch above it would have put two of everything on one panel and left the reader to work out which one was authoritative.

So this family draws none of that. It hands the chart card the selected period's data and adds the three things the chart card has no opinion about:

```
EarningsChartCard    the switch, the figure, the delta chip, the bars
tiles                the period's other readings
EarningsBreakdown    where the money came from
EarningsPayoutRow    when it reaches the bank
```

The tiles are `ai-profile-card`'s stat tile exactly — radius 10, padding 10, the reading over its label, one row from 640 and two columns below. A courier's "68 jobs done" and a profile's "9B lifetime tokens" are the same object, and a second tile geometry would be a second answer to one question.

The breakdown is `price-breakdown`'s `PriceSummary` under a heading, for the same reason. A courier's earnings are an ITEMISATION — jobs, tips, bonuses, adjustments, each a label and an amount the app formatted — which is a fare's itemisation seen from the other side. Writing a second list of label-and-amount rows here would give Bloom two of those, and only one of them would know about `estimated`, `pending`, the discount tone and the info popover.

## No total under the breakdown

Inside `EarningsSummary` the figure above the chart already IS the total, so `EarningsBreakdown` is given no `total` and draws the kinds with no rule. Pass `total` when you use the breakdown on its own.

## The money is never computed, and the headline is the proof

A bar carries BOTH a number and an amount, and they are not the same fact:

```tsx
{ label: 'Fri', value: 132.4, amount: '€132.40' }
```

`value` is GEOMETRY — how tall the bar is — and is never drawn. `amount` is what the reader sees.

`EarningsChartCard` formats its headline from a number, which is exactly what this family must not do. So the headline is CHOSEN rather than produced: the panel owns the chart's active bar, and `format` returns the period's own `total` at rest and the hovered bar's own `amount` over a bar. `earningsHeadline` is that choice, and it is a pure function so its boundaries — no bars, an index past the end, an index that outlived its data — are reachable by a test.

Two consequences, both deliberate:

- **The headline does not roll.** `ChartHeadline` counts a number up over 320ms; a pre-formatted amount has nothing to count. The 220ms fade still plays, so the change is still visible.
- **The Y axis prints a number.** A tick is a position on a scale the chart chose, not a sum the app made, so no string exists for it to have formatted. The default prints the rounded number with nobody's currency on it; **`formatAxisValue` is where an app puts its own**, and an app that forgets it gets a bare axis rather than a wrong currency.

## What jest cannot see, and where it is checked instead

`earningsHeadline` is pure, so the CHOICE is pinned in `src/__tests__/Earnings.test.tsx` at every boundary. The other half — that the panel actually owns the chart's active bar and re-reads the headline from it — is driven by pointer events on a plot surface that only exists after `onLayout`, and jsdom fires no layout. Measured: gutting `setActiveIndex` to a no-op left that whole suite green.

So it is `scripts/verify-earnings-headline.mjs`: a real Chrome, `page.mouse.move` over three bars of different value, reading the emitted headline. It also runs the case the pure test cannot reach — that no reading is ever the raw number the bar was SIZED by, which is what a `format` falling back to its argument would produce and what would still look plausible on screen. The axis words are checked the same way, and for the same reason.

## The period is controlled here, and the chart is remounted to follow it

`useChartRange` inside `chart-cards` is uncontrolled: it takes a `defaultRange` and there is no `range`. A period changed from outside the panel therefore cannot reach the chart card's own segmented control, and the switch and the panel would disagree with nothing to say so.

The chart is keyed on the period id, which remounts it with the new default. The cost is that the bars grow in again instead of morphing across the switch. **This is a gap in `chart-cards`, recorded rather than worked around silently** — the fix is a controlled `range` prop on `useChartRange`, and it is not made here.

## Payout states

Five, because "not in the account yet" is four different situations and a courier acts differently on each.

| state | tone | means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `scheduled` | default | waiting; nothing to do |
| `processing` | info | already moving; nothing to do |
| `paid` | success | done |
| `held` | warning | the reader has to do something, and the money is still coming |
| `failed` | error | the reader has to fix something |

**The state is said in words, not only in colour.** The badge carries the word and the tone is a second channel on top of it. `note` is drawn only for `held` and `failed` — a sentence on a scheduled payout is one nobody has to read.

The row is an `item`, not a card: `item` already owns the press target, the density, the slots and the disabled wash, and what this component decides is what goes in them.

## Traps

- **A period carries everything it says.** `total`, `bars`, `lines`, `stats` and `payout` are all the period's own, so switching the segment replaces the whole panel. A payout that does not change with the period is written once, at the panel level.
- **`deltaRatio` is a RATIO, not money.** `0.148` is up 14.8%. A percentage is not somebody's currency, so it is the one number here that is allowed to be formatted downstream.
- **A period id that names nothing falls back to the FIRST period**, never to nothing. An empty figure reads as "you earned nothing" rather than as "this app asked for a period that is not on the list".
