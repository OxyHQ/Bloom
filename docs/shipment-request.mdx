# Shipment request

```tsx
import {
  ShipmentRequestForm,
  ShipmentLoadPicker,
  ShipmentOptionsList,
} from '@oxy.so/bloom/shipment-request';

<ShipmentRequestForm
  route={{ stops, onSwap, onAddStop, onRemoveStop }}
  load={load}
  onLoadChange={setLoad}
  loadProps={{ notes: true }}
  photos={{ photos, onReorder, onRemove, onAdd }}
  options={{ extras, selectedExtras, onExtrasChange, accessOptions, access, onAccessChange }}
  price={{ lines, total }}
  footer={<Button variant="primary" size="large" fullWidth onPress={submit}>Ask for quotes</Button>}
/>
```

Three components: the **form** is the register and the order, the **load picker**
and the **options list** are the two halves of it that are this family's own.
Either half works on its own — a sheet that only asks for the load does not have
to mount a form.

## What the form draws, and what it does not

`ShipmentRequestForm` draws **nothing of its own**. Every section is an existing
Bloom family handed its props unchanged:

| section | component | subpath |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it goes | `RouteStops` | `@oxy.so/bloom/route-stops` |
| The load | `ShipmentLoadPicker` | this family |
| Photos | `SortablePhotoGrid` | `@oxy.so/bloom/sortable-media` |
| Options | `ShipmentOptionsList` | this family |
| Price | `PriceSummary` | `@oxy.so/bloom/price-breakdown` |

The sections are drawn with `FilterSection`, so the inset, the heading rhythm and
the rule between two sections are the ones a Bloom filter sheet already uses —
there is one set of numbers, not two.

The one decision the form makes is the **order**, and it is the order a carrier
needs the questions answered in: where, what, what it looks like, what changes
the price, what the price currently is. The price is last on purpose — the
options that move the number and the number itself end up on one screen.

**A section whose props are absent is not drawn.** `photos`, `options` and
`price` are each optional, and a job that needs no photograph should not be asked
for one. The route and the load are not optional: a shipment with neither is not
a request.

**The last section has no rule under it**, and the `footer` sits below it with
none either. A divider separates two things; there is nothing after the last one.

## The load picker

Four questions and an optional fifth, each drawn in the control that fits the
shape of its answer:

- **kind** — one answer out of five, each with a sentence explaining what it
  covers, so it is a column of selectable cards (the same card the listing editor
  builds its pickers from). Not chips: a chip has no room for the sentence, and
  the sentence is what stops someone filing a mattress under "parcel".
- **size** — a `SegmentedControl` of single letters, with the **chosen** rung's
  sentence under the control. Four sentences never fit in one control at phone
  width; a control whose every option carries its own sentence is a list.
- **weight** — a `TextField` whose unit is a `TextFieldSuffix`.
- **quantity** — a `StepperRow` from 1.
- **notes** — an auto-resizing `Textarea`, only when `notes` is set.

### The value arrives whole

`onValueChange` is called with the next `ShipmentLoad`, never with a patch. A
form that merges patches in five places has five chances to drop a field; this
one has none. It costs the caller a spread and buys a value that cannot be
half-applied.

### The unit is not part of the value

`weight` holds digits and at most one dot. `sanitizeWeight` accepts a comma and
emits a dot (half of Europe types one, and no app wants to parse both), strips
the unit, and turns a leading separator into `0.`. The "kg" beside the field is
drawn and announced; it is never stored. An app parses a number, not a sentence.

`isShipmentLoadComplete(load)` is the same decision the picker makes about
whether every control has been answered, exported so a submit button can be
disabled from it rather than from a second copy of it. It is **not** validation:
an app that prices a job has its own rules, and `errors` is where their messages
go.

## The options list

Three groups, and the control is the difference between them:

- **extras** are independent of one another, so each is a settings row with a
  `Switch` and its own price at the end. "And also this" is a switch.
- **access** — one of ground floor, stairs and lift is true, so they are a
  `radiogroup` of chips and choosing one unchooses the rest.
- **windows** — the same, over collection times.

**The interactive chips are `size="xl"`** — 32 drawn, 44 under a thumb through
`Chip`'s own `hitSlop`. The smaller rungs are for labels; a single-choice control
at phone width is not one.

**The row is not the control.** A pressable row wrapping a switch is two targets
for one answer, and on web the outer one is a button containing a checkbox. The
switch is the only thing that toggles.

**A switch draws no text**, so each one is named with its row's title, its
description and its price. A caption beside a switch is a sibling on both
platforms — neither associates the two — so a screen reader reading only the
control announces "switch, off" without this.

## Money

Every amount in this family is a **pre-formatted string**: `"+€12.00"`,
`"Included"`, `"−€3.00"`. Nothing here parses, sums, converts or rounds. The
running total is `PriceSummary`'s job, and the app computes it.

## Traps

- **`SHIPMENT_REQUEST_GEOMETRY` has no section numbers.** They belong to
  `FilterSection`, which draws them. A second copy here would drift from the one
  on screen.
- **The form measures nothing itself.** The load picker measures its own width
  (480) to decide whether the weight field sits beside the quantity; the form
  does not, because every section it draws already decides its own layout.
- **`kinds` is a prop, and the five defaults are opinions.** They are the five
  that change how a job is priced and carried, not a taxonomy of objects. There
  is deliberately no "Other": a marketplace that cannot name what it is carrying
  cannot quote it, and `notes` is where an unusual load is described.
