# Directions

```tsx
import { DirectionsSummary, DirectionsSteps } from '@oxy.so/bloom/directions';

<DirectionsSummary
  routes={routes}
  selectedRouteId={chosen}
  onSelectRoute={setChosen}
  stops={stops}
  onSwapStops={swap}
  modes={['drive', 'transit', 'walk', 'cycle']}
  mode={mode}
  onModeChange={setMode}
  onStart={navigate}
/>
<DirectionsSteps legs={legs} currentStepId={current} />
```

## What this family does NOT draw

- **The origin and the destination.** They are [`RouteStops`](/docs/route-stops), which
  already owns the marker column, the connector, the swap and the reached/current/pending
  states. `DirectionsSummary` renders it from `stops` and draws no second header. Without
  `stops` it draws no header at all, for an app whose screen already shows them.
- **The mode switcher.** A switcher of views is `SegmentedControl`. The four modes are
  data (`DIRECTIONS_MODE_LABELS`, `DIRECTIONS_MODE_ICON`), not four components.
- **A row.** Every route alternate and every turn step is an
  [`AddressRow`](/docs/address) — the row, the glyph tile, the press target, the density
  rungs, the trailing `meta` and the disabled opacity are `Item`'s, through it. This
  family adds a glyph table and the words.
- **A map.** There is no engine, no projection and no tile here.

## The duration is the figure

Nobody leaves the house because a place is 8.2 km away. `DirectionsSummary` leads with the
DURATION at `title-1`, tabular, under a quiet label — the figure block
[`ai-profile-card`](/docs/ai-profile-card) uses — with the distance as a `Chip` and the
traffic as a `Badge` on its tone beside it. The arrival time and the via line follow as two
quiet readings, each a 16 glyph and a line of secondary text.

`traffic: 'light'` is `success`, not `default`. A clear road is good news and the reader is
scanning for it; a neutral pill beside a red one reads as "no information" rather than as
"clear".

## The chosen route is not in the alternates

It is drawn in full above them, and a route shown twice is a reader wondering whether they
are two. That is also why the alternates are a `list` of pressable rows and not a
`radiogroup`: a radio group whose checked member is missing announces a choice with nothing
chosen. A press on a row CHOOSES it, which swaps it into the figure.

## The maneuver glyphs, and the two that are mirrored

A maneuver set is symmetric — a right turn IS a left turn seen from the other side — and
Bloom's icon set carries one side of each pair. `right` is `RiCornerUpLeftLine` mirrored
about its own centre (`scaleX: -1`), and `slight-left` is `RiArrowRightUpLine` mirrored.

**Mirroring is the correct answer here, not a shortcut.** The alternative is picking a
different glyph that happens to point the right way, and it does not share the partner's
stroke weight, arrowhead or corner radius — the pair then reads as coming from two icon
sets. A mirror moves nothing and needs no offset.

Each mirrored component is built ONCE at module scope. Built inside a render it would be a
new component type on every frame, and React would unmount and remount the SVG each time.

The table is `DIRECTIONS_MANEUVER_ICON`; the words are `DIRECTIONS_MANEUVER_LABELS`, and
they are announced BEFORE the instruction because a glyph says nothing aloud. That is the
same hole `RouteStops` fills for its marker shapes.

## The current step

It wears the accent's own `subtle` wash, painted through the row's `style` — **not through
`selected`**. `Item` maps `selected` on a row with no role to `aria-pressed`, and a turn
instruction is not a toggle: a screen reader would announce every step as "not pressed".

The state a step carries is "this is where you are", which ARIA has no attribute for on a
list row, so it travels in the NAME instead (`currentLabel`, default "Current step"),
where it is certain to be heard.

## Where a step's name goes

Exactly as in `RouteStops`, and for the same reason: a pressable step is a button and
carries its own name; a read-only one renders `role="none"`, which assistive technology is
told to ignore, so the name goes on the `listitem` around it. Naming both would read every
step twice.

## Transit

A leg header carries its mode glyph, its title at the section rung, its line badge and its
reading. `TransitLineBadge` is Bloom's `Badge` with two colours overridden and nothing
else — the same construction `listing-card`'s status pill uses, because an operator's line
colour is DATA the app owns and only the fill and the label may come from outside the
palette.

**The label is measured, not derived.** `readableOn` picks whichever end of the theme's own
reading pair clears more contrast against the operator's fill, in both modes — a derived
label (lighten the fill until it clears AA) invents a colour that is in no palette, and on
a saturated operator red it lands on pink. A line with no colour is the plain neutral
badge, which is the right answer for an operator that publishes none.

The badge shows two characters and says a sentence: `describeTransitLine` gives it
"Line L4, towards Pla del Bosc".
