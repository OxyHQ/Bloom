# Window

On a desktop an application's window has controls (close, minimize, zoom) in
its top leading corner, and no title bar: the content is the whole window and
is arranged around them. The platform's adapter says whether there is such a
window (the `window` capability of [platform surfaces](/docs/platform-surface)),
who draws its controls and where they are. Bloom keeps their room, draws them
where the platform asks, and makes the empty chrome move the window.

An application declares nothing but its shape. `AppShell` and `SettingsLayout`
do all of this themselves; the pieces below are for a layout of its own.

```tsx
import {
  useWindow,
  useWindowControlsRoom,
  WindowControls,
  WindowDragRegion,
} from '@oxy.so/bloom/window';
```

Without the capability (a browser tab, a phone, a desktop runtime whose
adapter reports none) every one of them is nothing at all: no controls, a room
of zero, a drag region that is a plain `View`.

## `useWindow()`

The window's state and the actions on it, or `null` without the capability:

- `active`, `maximized`, `fullscreen`, re-rendered as the platform reports them;
- `controls`: `bloom` (Bloom draws them, as on OxyOS) or `platform` (the
  platform draws its own, as a Mac does, and Bloom only keeps their room);
- `metrics`: the controls' box and their room, from the window's top and its
  leading edge, and the window's corner radius;
- `close()`, `minimize()`, `zoom()` (maximize or restore) and `startMove()`.

## `useWindowControlsRoom()`

`{ top, leading }`: the room the controls take, whoever draws them, and zero
without the capability or while the window is full screen. `leading` is the
left in a left-to-right locale and the right in a right-to-left one, so keep
it with `paddingInlineStart`, never `paddingLeft`.

## `useWindowHeaderRoom()`

In the region that is the window's chrome — the header of a `Screen` along
the window's top edge, or the content of a panel there with no `Screen` of its
own — the room its top bar keeps clear for the controls, from its own leading
edge; 0 everywhere else. `PageHeader` keeps it itself, inside its bar; a header
of an application's own keeps it with `paddingInlineStart` (see
[Panes as the title area](#panes-as-the-title-area)).

## `WindowControls`

Three bubbles of the surface material where `metrics.controls` puts them,
measured from the box it is mounted in, which must be the window's own corner
(the root of the window's content). The platform's numbers decide the
geometry: three targets spaced evenly across the controls' width, each a 14 px
disc in a 20 px target. On OxyOS the discs are 4 apart with their centres 26
from the top and the first 24 from the leading edge.

- On the active window they are tinted red, yellow and green (the `error`,
  `warning` and `success` tokens), on an inactive one neutral.
- The pointer over the group shows their symbols (×, −, and zoom's).
- A press shrinks a bubble to 88 % and springs it back as it acts. Reduced
  motion lands at once.
- Each is a button named "Close", "Minimize" and "Zoom" in the app's locale
  (`closeLabel`, `minimizeLabel`, `zoomLabel` win).
- `plate` floats them on a plate of the surface material, for full-bleed
  content (a photo, a video, a map) that keeps no room for them.

It draws nothing without the capability, where `controls` is `platform`, and
while the window is full screen.

## `WindowDragRegion`

A `View` that is the window's empty chrome: a press on it that no control
inside takes starts a move of the window (`startMove()`, from the press being
handled), and a second press within 500 ms zooms it. It asks for the press in
the responder system's bubble phase (`onStartShouldSetResponder`), which asks
the deepest view first, so a button, a field or any pressable inside takes its
own presses. Text and plain boxes take none, so a press on a toolbar's title
moves the window.

`disabled` makes it the plain `View`: a slot that is the window's chrome only
some of the time (a header in a full-screen window) keeps one tree, so its
content is never remounted.

## Who keeps the room

The corner has one owner. `AppShell` (see `windowControls` in
[App shell](/docs/app-shell)) and `SettingsLayout` (see
[Settings modal](/docs/settings-modal)) take it when they are the window's
content: they draw the controls and keep their room. A shell or a settings
layout inside one of them is a panel, away from the corner, and is laid out as
without windows.

A page that sets `AppShell header={null}` without panels owns the corner
itself when nothing else is there (no sidebar in flow, no top bar): its own
toolbar keeps `useWindowControlsRoom()` and is a `WindowDragRegion`.

### Panes as the title area

As in a desktop mail or notes application with panes and no sidebar, each
pane's toolbar is the window's title area. An `AppShell` with a panel layout
and nothing of its own at the corner (no sidebar, no top bar, no header) hands
the corner to its panels:

- **The panels fill the window**, rather than a reading envelope centred in
  it, so the first is at the corner, and they **start at the window's top
  edge**: the frame's top inset is 0 there, its start, end and bottom insets
  stay. Nothing moves by the room.
- **Each pane's header row is the window's toolbar**: as tall as twice the
  controls' centre line (`windowToolbarHeight(metrics)`, 52 on OxyOS, whose
  centres are 26 down), with no vertical padding and no safe area, so every
  title row is centred on the controls. The controls never move; the panes
  come to them.
- **Their top corners are concentric with the window's**: Bloom's concentric
  rule (the window's radius less the smaller gap beside the corner) with a top
  gap of 0 gives the window's own radius (`metrics.radius`, 16 on OxyOS), so a
  pane at the top leading corner has the window's curve there and nothing
  pokes out under the controls. The bottom corners keep the panel's radius.
  `ContentPanel` takes per-corner radii from `surfaceStyle` for its clip and
  its material.
- **The panel at the top leading corner** keeps the controls' room in its
  header: `room.leading` less where the panel already starts (the frame's
  start inset).
- **Every panel header along the window's top edge** is a drag region (side by
  side, a deck, retained panels whose box touches the top). Stacked, only the
  top one is. A collapsed layout's shown panel is at the corner. A floating
  panel is a dialog: none.
- **A layout `header`** spans the panels: it is the chrome, and the panels
  below it keep nothing.
- The controls are drawn once, by the shell. Full screen: none of this; the
  frame, bars and corners are as without windows. `windowControls="overlay"`:
  no room, no drag, no toolbar geometry.

The header is a panel route's `Screen` header (`header={<PageHeader …/>}`): the
`Screen` makes its header slot the drag region (as chrome it takes the presses
nothing inside takes, rather than letting them through) and `PageHeader` keeps
the room inside its own bar, so its background runs under the controls. The
room already holds the gap after the controls, so the bar starts at the larger
of its side inset and the room; a centred title clears it too. A header of an
application's own reads `useWindowHeaderRoom()`; a panel without a `Screen`
reads it in its content and wraps its own toolbar in a `WindowDragRegion`.

### A `Screen` as the window

A `Screen` that nothing else holds the corner for — no shell, no settings
layout, no outer `Screen`, no dialog or sheet (`ScreenScope`) — is the window's
content: it draws the controls in its corner (once; a `Screen` inside it is
not at the corner) and its header is the chrome above, with the whole
`room.leading`, the window's toolbar row at the window's top.

the concentric top corners; split, deck, collapsed, stacked,

the window, right to left, full screen without remounting the routes, and

(react-native-web's responder system and the bar's stylesheet).
