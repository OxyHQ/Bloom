# Vehicle picker

```tsx
import { VehiclePicker, VEHICLE_OPTIONS } from '@oxy.so/bloom/vehicle-picker';

<VehiclePicker
  value={vehicle}
  onValueChange={setVehicle}
  title="Which vehicle?"
  description="Pick the smallest one that can take the load."
  options={optionsForThisLoad}
/>
```

## Shape

A heading in the register a search panel's pickers use — `body-semibold` over
`body-2-regular`, 16 above the control — then a `radiogroup` of selectable cards,
12 apart. Omit `title` and `description` and the picker starts at its first
option, which is what a form section with its own heading wants.

Each card is the selectable card the listing editor's own pickers are built from:
a 44 icon tile, the vehicle's name at `headline-semibold`, its capacity under it,
and the radio indicator on the right. Under the card's hairline: the `fits` nouns
as outlined chips, then a `From · amount` line in tabular figures.

## One answer

A job goes in one vehicle, so the group is a `radiogroup` and each card a
`radio`. `value={null}` is a job that has not chosen yet — not an error, and not
a default guess.

## The detail is not a disclosure

Every card shows its capacity, its chips and its price **at rest**. The question
a vehicle picker answers is a comparison, and a card that reveals its numbers
only once chosen makes someone select four vehicles to choose one.

## Saying why

`disabled` dims a card and stops it. On its own that is a dead end: the row
someone wanted is greyed out and the screen does not say what to change. So a
disabled option draws `unavailableReason` on a warning-toned line under its
detail — and the same reason goes into the card's own **accessible name**, because
a reason drawn in a sibling line is never announced with the control.

Without a reason the picker falls back to `labels.unavailable`, which says only
*that* it is unavailable. That is the half that was already visible; write the
reason.

`disabled` on the picker itself dims every card, whatever each one says — it is a
constraint, and a card cannot re-enable itself.

## The ladder, and the one that breaks it

The five built-in vehicles are a ladder of capacity, smallest first: each takes
everything the one before it takes and more. That is what makes "the smallest one
that can carry this" a question the caller can answer.

`refrigerated` breaks the ladder on purpose. It is a van that holds a
temperature, so it is chosen for what the load **is** rather than for how big it
is, and it sits last rather than in weight order.

## Money

`priceFrom` is a **pre-formatted string** and is drawn exactly as given. Nothing
here adds, converts or rounds an amount. `VEHICLE_OPTIONS` carries no prices at
all — a price-from belongs to a marketplace and a moment, and a picker showing
invented amounts is worse than one showing the capacities alone. Spread an option
and add `priceFrom` where the app knows it.

## Traps

- **The picker measures itself, not the window.** Under 400 of its own width each
  card takes its compact inset — the same component is a phone column and a panel
  beside a map, and the window cannot tell those apart.
- **Two glyphs are borrowed.** Bloom's icon set has no truck and no van, so the
  van takes the bus glyph and the box truck takes a crate; the refrigerated van
  takes a snowflake rather than a second van outline, because what distinguishes
  it is the temperature and two near-identical van glyphs read as a drawing
  mistake. Pass `icon` per option to override any of them.
- **`onValueChange` is never called with the value already chosen.** Pressing the
  chosen card is a no-op, not a deselect: a required single choice that can be
  emptied by pressing it again is a control that loses answers.
- **`fits` is a chip row, not a list of features.** Three or four short nouns. A
  card carrying eight wraps into a paragraph and stops being scannable.
