# LocationPuck

`@oxy.so/bloom/location-puck`

Everything else in Bloom's maps set is chrome AROUND a map: a place card, a
route summary, the controls that float on top. This is the one thing drawn ON
it, and it answers one question — where are you, how sure are we, and which way
are you facing.

```tsx
import { LocationPuck } from '@oxy.so/bloom/location-puck';

<LocationPuck
  state="located"
  heading={heading}
  headingAccuracy={headingAccuracy}
  accuracyRadius={metresToPixels(accuracy)}
/>
```

## It positions nothing

The same contract `map-marker` publishes, and for the same reason: Bloom has no
projection, no tile source and no opinion about which map library you use. The
puck draws ONE square box with every layer centred in it, and the app centres
that box on the coordinate — the dot lands on the coordinate because it is at
the centre of the box, not because of an offset.

The box's size is whichever layer reaches furthest: `2 × accuracyRadius`,
`2 × coneLength`, or the puck itself. It takes no presses
(`pointerEvents="none"`), so a halo that happens to cover a third of the screen
never eats a pan the map was meant to receive.

`accuracyRadius` is in PIXELS, computed by the app from metres at this latitude
and zoom, and re-passed on every zoom. Without it no halo is drawn at all: a
halo of an invented size is a claim about precision nobody measured.

## The layers

| layer | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| halo | `MapAreaCircle` — literally the approximate-area circle `map-marker` publishes, at the radius the app gave |
| cone | a wedge from the dot, fading along its length, as wide as the device is unsure |
| puck | an 18 dot in the accent inside a 3 ring, `shadow-m`; a chevron while navigating |

The halo is not a second implementation of a translucent accent circle. It IS
the one `map-marker` already draws — the accent at 15% with a 1.5 edge — so a
preset change moves the halo with every other circle on the map.

## The cone is the uncertainty, drawn

`headingAccuracy` is the platform's own ± reading in DEGREES and it is the
cone's half-width, clamped into `[14°, 55°]`. Narrow means "this way"; wide
means "roughly this way"; there is no third vocabulary and no separate
confidence prop, because the device already reports the number.

`headingUnknown` draws **no cone** and speaks no bearing. A cone at a default
angle would be a confident claim about the one thing the device could not
measure, and a reader who cannot see the screen would be told a heading that
does not exist.

### The gradient is the trap

`react-native-svg` reads `stopColor` for its RGB and **discards any alpha inside
it**. A stop written as `rgba(…, 0.45)` renders correctly in a browser and
FULLY OPAQUE on a device — a hard-edged accent wedge lying over half the map —
and a gate that pinned both platforms to the same colour token would stay green,
because the two agree about every value and only the renderer's reading differs.
That exact defect shipped once in `theme/glass-colors.ts`.

So `LOCATION_PUCK_CONE_STOPS` holds an OFFSET and an OPACITY in separate fields,
and the cone hands `color` to `stopColor` and `opacity` to `stopOpacity`, which
are the two props SVG actually defines. `LocationPuck.test.tsx` asserts every
rendered `<stop>` carries a `stop-opacity` and that no `stop-color` contains an
alpha channel.

The gradient is also `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"`. The default,
`objectBoundingBox`, resolves against the bounding box of the element that
REFERENCES the gradient — the wedge, not the square — so `cx/cy = 0.5` lands
somewhere out along the cone and moves as the angle widens. Measured in a
browser before it was fixed: the fade started partway down the cone and was
brightest off to one side of it.

## `following`, `compass`, `navigating`

The mode decides how much the puck turns, and the component owns that decision
rather than making every caller repeat it:

- **`following`** — the map is north-up, so the cone turns by `heading`.
- **`compass`** — the app has already rotated the MAP so the heading is up, so
  the cone turns by **nothing**. Passing the heading here as well would apply
  the rotation twice; it is the mirror of the correction `MapCompass` makes for
  its needle.
- **`navigating`** — the dot becomes a chevron pointing along the route, and the
  cone disappears with it. The chevron IS the direction; a cone beside it is a
  second arrow saying the same thing.

The chevron has no drop shadow, on purpose. A drop shadow is the shadow of a
node's BOX, and the chevron's box is a square containing a wedge — so a shadow
on it draws a rectangle floating under a triangle. It is separated from the map
by its ring-coloured stroke, which traces the shape it actually has.

## The ring is the dot's own on-colour

A ring has one job: make the dot read as a deliberate object over tiles Bloom
cannot see. Bloom cannot measure anything against those tiles, but every fill in
the library already ships with the colour that reads ON it — so the live ring is
the accent's solid `foreground` (white for most seeds, black for a light one
like yellow or peach; the pair adapts, a hardcoded white ring does not), and the
stale ring is the map-chrome surface, because the stale dot is a text rung
floored against exactly that surface.

Measured over a dark tile: the chrome surface as the LIVE ring disappeared
entirely in dark mode — a dark ring on a dark map.

## States, and what they say out loud

| state | drawn | announced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `locating` | the dot swells 18%, the halo breathes | "Finding your location" |
| `located` | still | "Your location" |
| `stale` | the accent gone, the halo at half | "Your last known location" |

`stale` is the one that must be a sentence rather than a shade. A dimmer dot
tells a sighted reader something is different and tells everybody else nothing,
and "the map is showing where you were" is not recoverable by looking harder.

Every state adds the bearing when there is one — "Your location, facing 41
degrees" — and none of them adds it when there is not.

## Motion

Only `locating` moves, out of one shared driver: the dot swells and the halo
breathes over 1600ms. A pulse that never stops is a battery cost and a
distraction, so it stops the moment there is a fix, and `useReducedMotion()` —
or the `reducedMotion` prop — stops it outright.

The driver is written IMPERATIVELY from an effect and only READ in
`useAnimatedStyle`, with every shared value in the deps array. Both halves are
load-bearing on web: an animation returned from a mapper never ticks, and
without the worklets Babel plugin a mapper re-runs on its deps array rather than
on auto-tracked reads. See `docs/motion.mdx`.

Jest cannot see the pulse at all — the reanimated mock resolves a sequence to
its last value, so the driver reads 0 whether or not it was started. The suite
pins what IS asymmetric (an animating halo sits at the trough of its breath, a
still one at its resting opacity), and the motion itself is measured in a real
browser by sampling the dot's box over ten frames.

## What is not here

No engine, no tiles, no projection, no geolocation. `LocationPuck` is given a
state, a heading and a radius in pixels; it reads no sensor and subscribes to
nothing.
