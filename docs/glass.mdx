# Glass

`glass/GlassSurface.tsx` + `theme/glass-colors.ts`. Before Bloom 2.0 this material painted `Button`'s `primary` and `destructive` variants; `Button` now uses its own gradient recipe (`docs/button.mdx`). The measurements below were taken on that button and remain the reference for any glass surface.

## The material

`primary` and `destructive` are **tinted glass**: the brand colour at 85% over a `blur(10px)` of whatever is behind them, a hairline of the same colour at full strength, a lit white top rim and a soft drop shadow. The label is the fill's own on-colour, exactly as on the solid button — the pane IS the fill, so it carries the same label.

The glass is the sheen, not the wash. What reads as glass is the rim, the vertical gradient and the hairline; the 15% of the backdrop that shows through is a hint of depth, not the effect itself.

**One measured cost, stated because it is real.** The 15% bleed moves the pane toward the surface under it, which in light mode means lighter — the one direction a white label cannot afford. Of `primary`'s 640 preset × mode × surface combinations, **67 fall below WCAG AA**, in the band 4.21–4.50, all in light mode, on `acid-canopy`, `arctic-signal`, `blue`, `bronze-neon`, `clay-current`, `copper-field`, `electric-tide`, `faircoin`, `green`, `lagoon`, `lavender`, `malachite-rush`, `midnight-citrus`, `mint`, `olive`, `orange`, `pacific-flare`, `pine`, `plum`, `pumpkin`, `reef-pulse`, `rose`, `saffron-depth`, `sky`, `solar-flux`, `viridian-orbit` and `yellow`. Both solid Button fills pass all 1,280 rows; glass `destructive` is also unaffected (0/640, worst 5.32). The earlier low-chroma sweep reached zero failures at alpha 0.89; that historical sweep is retained below. The approved tonal surfaces improve the alpha 0.85 count from 73 to 67; the exact 67 row names are pinned in `tonal-glass-primary-failures.json`.

The 1.0.1 cohort now measures 36/340, including the original 18-preset cohort at 28/180; the 30 additions contribute 31/300. Before the approved tonal surfaces these counts were 38/340, 30/180 and 35/300. The current worst ratio improves from 4.17 to 4.21. These cohorts are asserted independently, so an unchanged total cannot hide a regression moving from one into the other.

If your app runs one of those 27 presets in light mode and needs AA on its primary button, use `variant="inverse"` for that call site, or raise `GLASS_FILL_ALPHA`.

Over a retained black/mid-grey/white media sample, **50 of 384** preset × mode × backdrop rows fall below AA, worst 3.61 (`destructive`: 0). The 1.0.1 catalog remains 25/204; the additions contribute 25/180, worst 4.17. That is a sample rather than a promise about arbitrary pixels. If a CTA must be legible over an image, `variant="inverse"` is opaque and backdrop-independent:

```tsx
<Button appearance="solid" tone="neutral">Comprar</Button>
```

`secondary`, `outline`, `ghost`, `text`, `link` and `icon` are unchanged: they have no fill for the tint to replace.

### On Android the blur is not applied, and it cannot be

Measured on an Android emulator (API 37): the pane paints its tint and the backdrop behind it is **not blurred**. `expo-blur`'s `blurMethod` defaults to `'none'` on Android, and `dimezisBlurView` additionally needs a `blurTarget` ref pointing at a `BlurTargetView` that wraps the content to blur.

The obvious fix — wrap the app in a `BlurTargetView` and hand the ref down — **crashes the app**. A `BlurView` that is a DESCENDANT of the view it points at makes the Android render tree recurse into itself: `SIGSEGV` in `RenderThread`, `RenderNode::prepareTreeImpl` repeating past 500 frames. Reproduced twice, isolated to one target and one pane. A glass `Button` fills its caller and therefore sits inside the content it would need to blur, so the only topology available to it is the one that crashes.

Three topologies, all measured on device (over 10dp stripes, where `std` is the discriminator — the MEAN is nearly identical in every case, so a mean-only comparison reports "no difference"):

| `BlurView` position | result |
| --- | --- |
| descendant of its own `blurTarget` | **native crash** |
| sibling of the target | blurs; std 0.70 vs 11.95 unblurred |
| inside a `Modal`, target in the root window | blurs; std 0.68, matches the blurred composite to 0.36/255 |

So Bloom asks for no blur method on Android at all. What that costs:

- Over Bloom's own flat surfaces — the documented backdrop range — nothing visible changes. A blur over a uniform colour is a no-op, so the painted pane matched the web composite to **0.75/255**, and every AA number above still holds.
- Over **textured** content (a photograph, a busy list) the Android pane transmits that texture at 15% instead of smoothing it. The pill clip, the hairline, the rim, the sheen and the drop shadow are all unaffected.

The third row is where an Android blur is still available, and Bloom now takes it — but only for surfaces inside a real RN `<Modal>`, which is the bottom-sheet path and not a `Button`. See `docs/dialog.mdx` for how to switch it on.

**So on Android, "glass" means tint, top sheen, hairline and drop shadow — plus a blur only where a target exists.** A glass `Button` has the first four and never the fifth.

iOS uses `UIVisualEffectView` and needs none of this, but it has not been verified on hardware.

### What a lower alpha would buy, and what it would cost

The recurring question about this material is whether it is transparent enough to be glass at all. `backdrop-filter` only acts on the fraction of the backdrop that gets **through** the pane, so at 0.85 the blur is running at full radius on 15% of the picture. Reproduce the whole trade with:

```bash
bun run storybook          # the DEV server; the script reads the theme through Vite
node scripts/measure-glass-alpha.mjs --url http://localhost:6006 --image docs/glass-alpha-sweep.png
```

![The same button at thirteen fill alphas, over a flat page, a photograph and a hard stripe pattern](./glass-alpha-sweep.png)

Measured in Chrome off painted pixels, with the label removed from the measurement clones so a white glyph cannot be mistaken for backdrop:

**Historical low-chroma surface sweep:** these measurements predate the approved tonal shell. The current alpha 0.85 primary count is 67/640; the other sweep rows have not been remeasured.

| alpha | backdrop response | blur delta over a photo (max/mean) | blur delta on a flat page | `primary` rows < AA | `destructive` rows < AA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.91 | 23.0 | 11 / 2.9 | 1 | 0 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| 0.90 | 25.0 | 12 / 3.1 | 1 | 0 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| 0.89 | 28.0 | 13 / 3.5 | 1 | 0 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| 0.88 | 31.0 | 15 / 3.8 | 1 | 8 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| 0.87 | 33.0 | 16 / 4.0 | 1 | 26 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| 0.86 | 36.0 | 17 / 4.4 | 1 | 49 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| **0.85** (shipped) | 38.0 | 18 / 4.7 | 1 | 73 / 640 | 0 / 640 |
| 0.75 | 63.7 | 31 / 7.8 | 1 | 431 / 640 | 128 / 640 |
| 0.65 | 88.7 | 43 / 10.9 | 1 | 492 / 640 | 640 / 640 |
| 0.55 | 114.7 | 55 / 14.2 | 1 | 531 / 640 | 640 / 640 |
| 0.45 | 139.3 | 67 / 17.4 | 0 | 580 / 640 | 640 / 640 |
| 0.35 | 165.3 | 79 / 20.6 | 1 | 635 / 640 | 640 / 640 |
| 0.25 | 190.3 | 91 / 23.8 | 1 | 635 / 640 | 640 / 640 |

Everything is out of 255. Backdrop response is how far the painted pane moves between a white and a black backdrop — linear in `1 - alpha`, at 25.4 ± 0.7 per 0.10 of alpha. Blur delta is how much the picture changes when the blur is switched off, which is the honest form of "can you see the blur".

Three things the table settles:

- **On a flat page the blur is worth ~1/255 at every alpha, including 0.25.** There is nothing behind the pane to blur, so lowering the alpha does not make a flat page glassy — it only makes the button paler. That is the whole of the "it doesn't look glassy" complaint, and no alpha fixes it.
- **Over a photograph the blur is already visible at 0.85** (18/255 peak). Where there is something to see, the shipped material is doing the thing.
- **Legibility falls off a cliff immediately below 0.85.** One step down takes `primary` from 73 to 431 failures and `destructive` from 0 to 128; two steps down puts every one of `destructive`'s 640 rows under AA.

So there is no single alpha that both matches the reference and reads as glass on a flat surface. That is a property of the material — a blur needs a backdrop — not a constant nobody has tuned yet. To make a specific surface read as glass, put something behind it.


## The chrome role

Everything above prices a **brand** fill: a `primary`-coloured pane over one of Bloom's own five neutral surfaces, labelled with that fill's on-colour. A page header's island is the other case, and almost nothing about it is the same:

| | accent | chrome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fill | the caller's brand token | rung 1 of the surface ladder — the fill a card and a menu panel paint |
| label | the fill's own on-colour | the theme's own `text` |
| backdrop | Bloom's five neutral surfaces | anything: a photograph, a video still, a map |
| alpha | 0.85 | 0.72 light · **0.80 dark** |

Inside Bloom — `theme/glass-colors.ts` is not a published subpath, for the same reason `resolveGlassColors` is not:

```tsx
import { resolveSurfaceLevel } from '../styles/surface-levels';
import { resolveChromeGlassColors } from '../theme/glass-colors';

const level = resolveSurfaceLevel(theme, 1);
const glass = resolveChromeGlassColors(level.background, level.border, theme.isDark);
```

Both arguments are resolved colour strings the caller already holds, because the caller is the only one that knows which rung of the ladder its container sits on.

**Dark carries the higher alpha**, which is the opposite of the intuition that a dark UI can afford more transparency. The worst case for each mode is the backdrop it is furthest from: a light pane is washed *down* over black and barely moves, because it is already near white; a dark pane over white has the whole luminance range above it to be washed out into.

Because the backdrop is unknown, the range measured is the **whole** range — pure white and pure black. Every composite a real backdrop can produce lies between them on every channel, so a pane that clears AA at both ends clears it everywhere between.

Measured over 64 presets × 2 modes × 2 endpoints × 2 platforms (512 rows), with the theme's own `text` as the label:

| | failures below AA | worst ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| light | 0 / 256 | **7.84** (`sky`, over black, web) |
| dark | 0 / 256 | **6.47** (`graphite`, over white, web) |

and the pane still moves **49.98–69.97** of 255 between a black and a white backdrop, so it is glass rather than a tinted slab. Web is the worse of the two platforms in both modes: `expo-blur`'s own tint adds body on native that CSS `backdrop-filter` does not.

Gate: `src/theme/__tests__/chrome-glass.test.ts`, which pins the two floors to the hundredth and the translucency range with **literal** bounds. A floor derived from the alpha constant would move with it, so an opaque material would satisfy its own floor.

## Islands

`glass/GlassIsland` is the assembly: the material, the hairline, the geometry and the drop shadow, in the order that works. `GlassSurface` deliberately owns none of the last three — it clips itself, and a shadow drawn on a clipping node is clipped away on iOS — so every caller that wants a floating pane would otherwise repeat the same four steps.

An island also mounts a `ControlSurface` with `material: 'glass'`, so controls inside it paint flush. That is the material's rule rather than a preference: a translucent pane inside a translucent pane composites two alphas and two blurs for one pane's worth of depth, and on Android the second blur is not available at all.

Internal (`glass/` is not a published subpath). It is reached through `ButtonGroup`'s `glass` variant and `PageHeader`'s floating presentation.

### On a device: tint, and no blur

Historical measurement before the tonal-shell native intensity adjustment below, on a **Pixel 8a** over a hard 10dp stripe pattern, sampling the same rows inside a chrome island and on the bare stripes beside it:

| backdrop stripe | bare | inside the island |
| --- | --- | --- |
| white | 255 | 254 |
| black | 0 | 210 |

The backdrop contributes **44 of 255** — the pane is translucent — and the stripe **edge stays sharp**, where a blur would smear a 26px transition. That is the Android behaviour this file describes: `expo-blur` is asked for no blur method, so it paints its tint and nothing else. Nothing in `logcat` asked for one or reported a crash.

The composite works out at ≈**0.83** opacity against the web pane's 0.72 plus sheen. The difference is `expo-blur`'s own tint — one `intensity` drives it together with the radius and a caller cannot decline it — which is the platform gap `GLASS_BLUR_INTENSITY` documents, appearing exactly where it said it would.

The ramp `PageHeader` draws over the same material was checked for the `stopColor` alpha trap at the same time: **0 achromatic rows of 311** in both modes, so the hue survives the SVG renderer.

### Tonal-shell material verification

The approved chroma28 shell retains the web's 10px blur and fill alpha 0.85. Native
expo-blur intensity is now 30 instead of 50: its unavoidable neutral tint otherwise
produced a 6.478/255 painted-channel gap against web on saturated surfaces. The
new maximum is 3.887/255, inside the unchanged <4 gate and still measurably nonzero.
This reduces native blur strength; it does not claim equal spatial blur across
platforms. Sheen, fill opacity and hairline remain unchanged. Device blur behavior
is subject to the Android topology limitations documented above.

Across 3840 preset/mode/tone/surface combinations, on-fill labels now fail AA in 480
cases and page-text labels in 2577. The earlier catalog contributes 258 and 1377;
the additional 30 presets contribute 222 and 1200. Exact equality gates retain these
measured limitations rather than reducing the AA requirement.

Chrome islands retain zero AA failures over the black/white backdrop endpoints:
the new light minimum is 7.84 (`sky/light/black/web`), and dark 6.47
(`graphite/dark/white/web`). Previously pinned floors were 8.23 and 6.43. These
are measured at unchanged chrome alphas 0.72/0.80 and now pin exact named rows.
