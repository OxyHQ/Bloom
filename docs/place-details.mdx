# Place details

```tsx
import {
  PlaceAmenities,
  PlaceHours,
  PlaceInfoList,
  PlacePopularTimes,
  PlaceTransit,
} from '@oxy.so/bloom/place-details';
```

[`PlaceCard`](/docs/place-card) at `density="detail"` draws the HEADER of a place — the
photo, the name, the rating, the figure and the four actions. This family is everything
under it. Together they are the screen a maps app opens when a result is picked; neither
half is a screen on its own.

## What composes the screen

| the part | what draws it |
| --- | --- |
| the header | `PlaceCard density="detail"` |
| the hours | `PlaceHours` |
| the photos | [`ListingPhotoGrid`](/docs/listing-details) + [`ZoomableMediaGallery`](/docs/zoomable-media-gallery) |
| the address, phone, website | `PlaceInfoList` |
| how busy it is | `PlacePopularTimes` |
| what it has | `PlaceAmenities` |
| the stops around it | `PlaceTransit` |
| the reviews | [`place-reviews`](/docs/place-reviews) |
| the section frames | [`ListingSection`](/docs/listing-details) |

The sections are `ListingSection` and not a `PlaceSection`: a home's detail page and a
place's detail screen stack sections the same way — a hairline, 32 above and below, a
title at `title-3-semibold` — and a second component for that is a second set of numbers
to keep equal.

```tsx
<ScrollView>
  <PlaceCard density="detail" {...place} actions={actions} />
  <PlaceHours state="open" summary="Open until 20:00" days={week} />

  <ListingSection title="About">
    <PlaceInfoList items={info} />
  </ListingSection>

  <ListingSection title="Popular times">
    <PlacePopularTimes days={popular} />
  </ListingSection>
</ScrollView>
```

On a phone that goes inside a `BottomSheet` over the map; on a desktop inside a
`ContentPanel` beside it. Nothing in this family knows which — every block reads the
surface it was dropped on (`styles/surface-levels.ts`), so a sheet that publishes its own
fill gets a settings card, a chart track and hairlines resolved against the sheet rather
than against the page:

```tsx
<SurfaceLevelProvider level={1} fill={colors.card}>
  <View style={[{ backgroundColor: colors.card }, surfaceFillVars(colors.card)]}>
    …
  </View>
</SurfaceLevelProvider>
```

## `PlaceInfoList` is `settings-list`, row for row

A place's contact block and a settings screen are the same object: a short card of rows,
each carrying one value and one thing that happens when you press it. So this is
`SettingsListGroup` and `SettingsListItem` — the glyph well, the 44 minimum height, the
hairline between rows, and the surface the group resolves from whatever is behind it.

Two decisions of its own:

- **The VALUE is the first line and the kind is the second.** A reader scanning a place
  sheet is looking for the address, not for the word "Address". The announcement inverts
  that — "Address: Plaça de les Bruixes 4" — because a list of five rows is *listened* to
  by kind, and five values with no labels cannot be told apart.
- **The trailing glyph is not a button.** `action` picks it (a copy sheet, a handset, an
  arrow out of the box) so the press is legible before it is made, and the row stays ONE
  press target with ONE announcement. A second control inside a pressable row is a second
  tab stop saying the same word, and on web it is a button inside a button.
- **The action's WORD is in the name, not in a hint.** The first version put it on
  `accessibilityHint`, and react-native-web drops that prop entirely — no `title`, no
  `aria-description`, no `aria-describedby` — so on web the row announced a fact and never
  said it could be pressed, while native read it correctly. Same class as
  `accessibilityState`, same fix: the name is the one spelling that reaches both. It goes
  last ("Address: Plaça de les Bruixes 4, Copy"), because the fact is what the reader came
  for. `PLACE_INFO_ACTION_LABELS` holds the English words; `actionLabels` replaces them.

`action` is a closed list rather than a slot for the same reason: one row, one action.
Nothing here touches the clipboard, the dialler or the browser — `onPress` is handed the
row's id and the app does the rest.

`numberOfLines` defaults to **2**, because a postal address does not fit on one across a
phone and the tail that would be cut is the part that identifies it. A settings row's
title is a noun and fits on one; this one's title is the row's content.

## `PlaceHours` says what the app tells it

**Nothing here reads a clock.** `summary` is the app's whole sentence ("Open until 20:00",
"Closes soon", "Closed — opens 07:30 tomorrow") and `today` is the app's flag. A place's
hours are in the place's timezone, the reader is in another, and whether a Tuesday is open
also depends on a holiday calendar Bloom has never heard of. A component that guessed would
be wrong twice a year, quietly.

The state pill and the state word are `PlaceCard`'s own — `PLACE_OPEN_TONE` and
`openLabelFor` — so a place cannot be "Open" on the header of a sheet and "Closing soon" in
the body of it.

The disclosure is Bloom's `Accordion`: it owns the chevron, the `aria-expanded` and the
reveal. One trap comes with it — `AccordionContent` keeps its children mounted while shut
(it animates `maxHeight` to 0), so the week would still be read out under a trigger that
says it is collapsed. `PlaceHours` hides its week from assistive tech while shut; nothing
in there takes focus, so it costs nothing.

**A split day stays on one line** (`07:30 – 14:00, 17:00 – 20:00`). The week is read as a
column of days, and a day that takes two rows breaks the alignment that makes the column
scannable. Both stretches are short, and the pair fits where one does.

A day with no `intervals` is CLOSED — that is the whole encoding, so an app cannot say
"closed" and list hours at the same time. `exception` puts a `warning` badge on the row
("Public holiday", "Market day") and adds it to the row's announcement, because a badge is
silent.

## `PlaceAmenities` is the listing amenity block, called

`layout="list"` **is** [`AmenityList`](/docs/listing-details), handed the same items — not
a copy of it and not a port. Icon, label, an optional second line, a struck-through label
for what the place does not have, the two-column split from 560 wide and the "Show all"
button are all that component's. The only difference between a home's amenities and a
place's was the noun, and a noun is not a component.

`layout="chips"` is the one thing a listing page does not have: a wrapping strip of `Chip`s
for the top of a sheet, where four attributes have to read at a glance. Same items, same
unavailable treatment, different density.

The name sits on the strip's `listitem` and not on the chip, because a non-pressable `Chip`
is a plain `View` — and on web a `div` carrying an `aria-label` and no role is not
announced at all.

## `PlacePopularTimes` is a chart

A meter answers "how full is this one thing". Every reading here only means something
against the twenty-three beside it, so it is a chart, drawn the way Bloom draws charts:
`react-native-svg` bars on `chart-cards`' own bar geometry (`singleBarSlot`,
`roundedBarPath`) in one of its nine series hues, over its chart track colour.

**Which chart card this is not.** `StepsCard` draws exactly these bars — inside the medical
card's fixed height, under its own headline, its week pill and its count-up, with seven
labelled bands. A place sheet already has a section header and needs twenty-four bands with
a day switch, so reusing that card would have meant suppressing four of its five parts. The
bar maths is what was worth sharing, and that is what is imported.

Three decisions:

- **The domain is a fixed 0–100.** A per-day maximum would make Tuesday's quiet peak as
  tall as Saturday's queue, which is the one comparison the chart exists to allow.
- **The track is drawn behind every hour**, so a quiet hour is still a shape and a CLOSED
  hour reads as a gap rather than as a missing one.
- **`currentHourIndex` belongs to a DAY.** Switching to Tuesday drops the "now" marker and
  the live line with it, because Tuesday has no now.

The chart is ONE image with one name ("Wednesday, busiest at 9, now 18, busier than usual").
Twenty-four bars two pixels wide would be twenty-four tab stops saying a number each, which
is not a reading of anything. The axis labels every hour that fits and every second or third
below that — twenty-four labels across a phone is sixteen pixels each — and the current
hour keeps its label whatever the step.

`PLACE_BUSY_LABELS` holds the three English trend sentences; `trendLabel` replaces one.

## `PlaceTransit` reuses the line badge

The line pills are `directions`' own [`TransitLineBadge`](/docs/directions), imported. An
operator's line colour is data the app owns, and that badge already knows how to *measure* a
readable label against an arbitrary fill in both modes. A second badge here would be a second
answer to that question, and they would drift.

A departure is ONE utterance — "Line L4 to Pla del Bosc, 4 min, live" — because read part by
part, a column of three badges and three times is six announcements the listener has to pair
up themselves.

**`realtime` is a colour AND a word.** The dot and the green time are what a sighted reader
sees; `live` is in the row's name for everyone else. Green alone says nothing to a reader who
cannot see it, and it is the first thing a high-contrast mode takes away.

The stop header is pressable only when `onPressStop` is given (an app pans its map to the
stop). It takes the library's one hover wash and one focus ring, built by
`interactiveWebCss` rather than hand-written.

## Geometry

`PLACE_DETAILS_GEOMETRY` holds the numbers: 16 between blocks, 12 between rows, a 20 glyph,
a 120 plot with a 22 axis under it, a 4 bar radius. The gaps are `listing-details`' own
rungs, so a place's section and a home's section stack identically; only the chart has
measurements of its own, because nothing else in Bloom draws a day of hours.
