# MapAttribution

`@oxy.so/bloom/map-attribution`

The quietest thing on the map, and the one part of it that is not optional. Tile
providers require a credit; a map without a scale cannot be read as a
measurement; and a reader deciding whether to trust what they are looking at
wants to know when it was last refreshed.

```tsx
import { MapAttribution } from '@oxy.so/bloom/map-attribution';

<MapAttribution
  credit="Map data © Open Map Project contributors"
  scales={[{ width: 88, label: '500 m' }, { width: 108, label: '2000 ft' }]}
  updated="Updated 12 March"
  onPressCredit={openLicence}
/>
```

## The credit is required and never defaulted

Every other string in this family has a sensible English default. This one does
not, and that is the point: a component that invented a credit would put a
WRONG attribution on a map, which is a licence problem rather than a visual one.
The app passes what its provider's licence says, verbatim.

It also never truncates. The row wraps instead — a credit that silently lost its
second half on a narrow phone is the same failure as not drawing it.

With `onPressCredit` the credit becomes `Button`'s `link` variant at the same
type step: running text with the library's own focus ring and hover underline,
rather than a second pressable with a ring of its own. Give it a `creditLabel`
when the drawn words are not a sentence ("© OMP").

## Legibility over content Bloom cannot see

The map can be a night satellite tile, a white street map or a photograph, so no
text rung can be floored against it. The strip sits on a `GlassIsland` instead —
a translucent pane whose fill is rung 1 of the surface ladder, a surface Bloom
DOES own — and every rung here is measured against THAT.

`variant="inline"` drops the pane and draws the words and nothing else, with no
padding at all, for a strip already inside a surface the app painted. That is
also how `MapScaleBar` is mounted inside `MapAttribution`: one pane for the whole
strip, never a pane inside a pane.

The rungs:

| part | rung | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| credit | `textSecondary` (7:1) | 11px, and a licence requirement |
| updated | `textTertiary` (4.5:1) | quieter, still AA |
| scale rule | `textGraphical` (3:1) | a LINE, not text |

Flooring the rule at text contrast would make the quietest thing on the map the
loudest thing on the strip.

## MapScaleBar

A measured span with a tick at each END, and what that span is worth. The ticks
are what make it read as a measurement between two marks rather than as an
underline.

Two bars — metric over imperial — share a left edge and stack, so the eye
compares them as one instrument rather than reading two.

**It converts nothing.** Both the width in pixels and the formatted words come
from the app, because only the app knows its projection: which round number of
metres a given number of pixels is worth at this latitude and this zoom. A scale
bar that guessed its own units would be a measuring instrument that measures the
component rather than the map.

The whole bar is one `img` named `"Scale, 500 m, 2000 ft"`. A bar and two ticks
say nothing aloud, and "500 m" on its own is as likely to be a distance to
somewhere as a scale.

## What is not here

No clock. `updated` is a string the app formatted — this component does not know
what "3 days ago" means, and a component that computed it would be wrong for
every locale it was not written in.
