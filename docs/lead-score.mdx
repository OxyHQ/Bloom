# LeadScoreCard

```tsx
import { LeadScoreCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom';

<LeadScoreCard
  score={82}
  accessibilityLabel="Lead score for Larkspur Freight"
  valueText="82 of 100, hot"
  factors={[
    { label: 'Fits the ideal profile', contribution: 24, detail: 'Logistics, 200–500 people' },
    { label: 'No decision-maker identified', contribution: -9 },
  ]}
  trend={{ label: '+8 against last week', direction: 'up' }}
/>
```

## Nothing here is hand-rolled

The ring is `stat-bar`'s `MeterRing` and every factor bar is its `Meter`. That
is not a stylistic preference: those two own the geometry, the neutral rail and
the FLAT `aria-value*` props, and seventeen hand-rolled copies in this repo
disagreed about all three before they existed.

The accessibility half is the one that matters most here. react-native-web reads
`aria-*` and never `accessibilityValue`, so a ring that set only the object
announces its role and no value — in a screenshot, in a browser and in a
prop-level test it looks perfect. `LeadScore.test.tsx` reads the emitted
`aria-valuenow` / `aria-valuemin` / `aria-valuemax` / `aria-valuetext` instead.

## The name is a required prop

`accessibilityLabel` is required at the type level. ARIA computes no name for a
`progressbar` from its contents, and the ring draws a bare number — so without
it the card announces "progress bar, 82%" and never says whose lead it is. A
heading beside the ring does not name it either; that is a SIBLING.

`valueText` is what a screen reader says instead of the raw number: without it
`82` of `100` is announced as a percentage, which is right by accident here and
wrong the moment `max` is 5.

## The band is a tone

`cold` / `warm` / `hot` map to the `info` / `warning` / `success` tones in
`LEAD_SCORE_BAND`, and every colour comes from `resolveAccentColors`. The card
names no red and no orange: those would be right in one preset and wrong in the
other sixty-three, and in dark mode they would be wrong in all of them.

**`hot` is the SUCCESS tone, not the error one.** A hot lead is the good
outcome; reading temperature as danger is exactly the confusion a tone
vocabulary exists to prevent.

The band is DERIVED from the score as a fraction of `max`
(`LEAD_SCORE_THRESHOLDS`: warm at 0.4, hot at 0.7), so a 0–100 score and a 0–5
one land in the same places. Boundaries are inclusive upward — exactly 0.4 is
warm — because a score sitting on a threshold has reached it. An app with its
own thresholds passes `band` and skips the derivation; `bandLabel` renames the
pill without changing the tone.

A non-positive `max` has no scale to divide, so the band is `cold` rather than
`NaN`: the card renders data it did not choose instead of throwing over it.

## The factors

Each factor is a signed contribution. The BAR measures the magnitude; the sign
lives in the text beside it and in the tone of the fill — positive in the
positive tone, negative in the negative one.

**All the bars share one scale**: the largest absolute contribution in the set,
floored at 1 (`factorScale`). Normalising each bar to itself would draw a `+2`
and a `+30` at the same length, which is the one thing a row of bars is read
for. Each bar announces its signed value as `aria-valuetext` ("−9 points"), so
the sign is not carried by colour alone.

A zero factor draws an empty bar rather than being hidden: "this counted for
nothing" is a fact about the lead.

## The trend

`trend` is a pre-formatted label plus a direction. The card does no arithmetic
and does not know what "last period" means — a week, a quarter, since the deal
opened — so it draws what it is handed with the direction's arrow and tone.
`flat` is the neutral tone, not a quiet negative.
