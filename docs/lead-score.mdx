# LeadScoreCard

```tsx
import { LeadScoreCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom';

<LeadScoreCard
  score={82}
  accessibilityLabel="Lead score for Larkspur Freight"
  valueText="82 of 100, hot"
  factors={[
    { label: 'Fits the ideal profile', contribution: 24, detail: 'Logistics, 200–500 people' },
    { label: 'No decision-maker', contribution: -9, detail: 'One contact, no budget owner' },
  ]}
  trend={{ label: '+8 against last week', direction: 'up' }}
/>
```

## Two panels in one card

It is drawn the way Bloom draws a SCORE — `chart-cards`' `SleepScoreCard` is the
register, and the `BesideTheReference` story puts the two in one shot at the
same width:

| panel | what it holds |
| --- | --- |
| header | washed in the band's tone: the quiet `body-medium` label over the `title-1-medium` verdict with the band's glyph, the trend as a quiet icon-and-label line, and the ring — 132 across, CENTRED, the score at `display-4-medium` inside it |
| factors | neutral, padded on the LEFT only so the rules run to its right edge: one row per factor — a 10 mark, the label and its detail as one reading line, and the signed contribution right-aligned and tabular |

The card itself is 10 padding and a 10 gap; both panels take `radius-16` inside
its `radius-20`.

## There are no bars

Every factor used to be a `Meter`. Five bars stacked down a card is a chart
pretending to be a measurement — a CHART colours by WHICH datum it is, and these
are all the same datum at different sizes — and it was the single thing that
made this card read as a dashboard from another product rather than as Bloom.

A contribution is a NUMBER. It is printed (`+24`, `-9`), it is tabular so the
column lines up, and the MARK beside the label says which way it pulls: the
accent for a factor that pushes the score up, the quiet graphical neutral for
one that pulls it down. Five bars also announced `progressbar` five more times;
`LeadScore.test.tsx` now asserts that the card emits exactly ONE, and that it is
the ring.

The one measurement on the card is the score, and it is a ring, because there is
one of it.

## The ring

`MeterRing` from `stat-bar` owns the geometry and the FLAT `aria-value*` props.
The arc is the meter's own accent fill whatever band the score falls in: that is
what a meter means in Bloom — how much of one thing there is. A green ring
claims "healthy", which is not the claim "82 of 100" makes.

**The TRACK is read off the header wash, not from `stat-bar`'s default rail.**
That rail is a neutral computed for the page; on a tinted panel it reads as a
foreign grey ring rather than as the empty part of this one. `resolveLeadScorePaint`
takes it from `hairlineOn(theme, header)`.

It is 132 across, centred, with the number at `display-4-medium` — larger than
the verdict word beside it. At 72 in a corner it was a widget, and the one fact
the card exists to state was smaller than the label.

## The tint is FLATTENED before anything is derived from it

The header is `resolveAccentColors(colors, tone, 'subtle').background` —
composited over the card, not painted raw.

The `*Subtle` members are `rgba()` on purpose, and painting one renders
correctly. What does not work is DERIVING off it: every reader in
`styles/color-contrast.ts` treats a colour as opaque, so a text rung or a
hairline read from `rgba(16 185 129 / 0.24)` is computed against solid emerald
and lands on a panel that is nothing like it. Measured here before the flatten:
the header's secondary label came back at **1.96:1** against its own panel in
dark mode, and the card rendered perfectly.

So `flattenOver` composites the tint ONCE (parse-and-re-emit, the same move
`chip/hue-colors.ts` makes for a translucent hue), the panel paints the
flattened colour, and every rung is read off that — the two can then never
disagree. Flattened, the worst secondary label over the whole
`preset × mode × band` sweep measures 6.1:1.

Both panels publish themselves with `SurfaceLevelProvider` and
`surfaceFillVars`, so anything nested inside either one steps off the right
parent.

## The band is the VERDICT, and it is the tint

`cold` / `warm` / `hot` map to the `info` / `warning` / `success` tones in
`LEAD_SCORE_BAND`. The tone paints the header wash and the band's glyph; the
word beside it is the reading colour of that wash. A pill saying the same word
would say it twice.

**`hot` is the SUCCESS tone, not the error one.** A hot lead is the good
outcome; reading temperature as danger is exactly the confusion a tone
vocabulary exists to prevent.

The band is DERIVED from the score as a fraction of `max`
(`LEAD_SCORE_THRESHOLDS`: warm at 0.4, hot at 0.7), so a 0–100 score and a 0–5
one land in the same places. Boundaries are inclusive upward — exactly 0.4 is
warm — because a score sitting on a threshold has reached it. An app with its
own thresholds passes `band`, which moves the WASH with it; `bandLabel` renames
the verdict without changing the tone.

A non-positive `max` has no scale to divide, so the band is `cold` rather than
`NaN`: the card renders data it did not choose instead of throwing over it.

## The name is a required prop

`accessibilityLabel` is required at the type level. ARIA computes no name for a
`progressbar` from its contents, and the ring draws a bare number — so without
it the card announces "progress bar, 82%" and never says whose lead it is. A
heading beside the ring does not name it either; that is a SIBLING.

`valueText` is what a screen reader says instead of the raw number: without it
`82` of `100` is announced as a percentage, which is right by accident here and
wrong the moment `max` is 5.

react-native-web reads `aria-*` and never `accessibilityValue`, so a ring that
set only the object announces its role and no value — in a screenshot, in a
browser and in a prop-level test it looks perfect. `LeadScore.test.tsx` reads
the emitted attributes instead.

## The factors

Each factor is a signed contribution. `factorLine` joins the label and its
`detail` into ONE string, because the row truncates as a sentence: two
independently shrinking texts drop the label to "Fits the ide…" while the detail
beside it still has room.

A zero factor draws a row with `0` in it rather than being hidden: "this counted
for nothing" is a fact about the lead. The signed number is printed in the
READING colour, not a status one — the sign is what says which way it went, and
a red `-9` beside a grey mark would be two spellings of one fact.

`factorsLabel` is the panel's header row, above the first factor and sharing its
rule.

## The trend

`trend` is a pre-formatted label plus a direction. The card does no arithmetic
and does not know what "last period" means — a week, a quarter, since the deal
opened — so it draws what it is handed with the direction's arrow, as a quiet
line on the wash. `LEAD_SCORE_TREND` carries the glyph and nothing else: the
arrow is the direction, and the card keeps one tinted thing rather than three
competing ones.
