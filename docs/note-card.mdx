# NoteCard

A note, previewed. It is the cell of a board, the row of a list, and the thing a
bulk action is applied to — one component, because those are the same note.

```tsx
import { NoteCard } from '@oxy.so/bloom';

<NoteCard
  title="Harbour walk — what to bring"
  excerpt="Slack water is at 06:40, which gives us about forty minutes…"
  tags={['field notes', 'tide']}
  meta={{ edited: '2 min ago', notebook: 'Field notes', attachments: 2 }}
  pinned
  onPress={open}
/>
```

## The two densities are one component

|  | `grid` | `row` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| padding | 16 | 12 |
| radius | `radius-12` | `radius-8` |
| title | 2 lines | 1 line |
| excerpt | 4 lines | 1 line |
| checklist preview | 4 items | 2 items |
| tags | their own row | folded into the metadata line |
| metadata | last line | inline after the excerpt |
| min height | — | 72 |

`grid` is the board: a masonry cell where the body is what you are scanning.
`row` is the list: the title is what you are scanning and everything else is a
trail after it.

They are a `density` prop rather than two components on purpose. Everything but
the arrangement — which fields are drawn, how the tone is composited, what
selection means — is the same decision, and two components is where two answers
to one of those grow.

## Selection is two different things, so it has two spellings

`selected` on its own means **this is the note you are reading**: the card takes
the tone's edge and announces `aria-current`. The reader did not choose it out of
a set; they are at it.

`selectable` turns the list into **bulk selection**: the card IS a checkbox —
`role="checkbox"`, both spellings of `checked`, a box in the leading slot, and a
press toggles instead of opening. `onPress` is not called while `selectable` is
set, which is the whole point of the mode.

**A card with no handler and no `selectable` is not a control at all** — it
renders as a plain `View` with the composed name and no role. A `Pressable` with
its handlers left off still lands in the tab order and still announces "button",
and neither is true of the thing on screen.

The box in the leading slot is `checkbox/shared`'s own `CheckboxGlyph`, hidden
from assistive technology and non-interactive: the CARD carries the role and the
press, so the box is the drawing of that state rather than a second control to
tab to.

## A checklist note previews as a checklist

Pass `checklist` and it replaces `excerpt`. Flattened into text, a checklist
reads as a paragraph of fragments — "Finish part two Borrow the essays Return the
overdue ones" — which is worse than no preview.

The rows are read-only. Each announces its own state (`role="checkbox"`,
`aria-checked`, `aria-disabled`) and is named by its text, because a tick with no
name is a decoration. `checklistTotal` is what the "+N more" line counts against,
so a card can be handed the first four items of nine.

## The tone is composited, never appended

`tone` is one of Bloom's accent tones and there is no hex here on purpose: a note
colour picked by eye is the one that disappears in dark mode.

The mechanism is the part worth knowing. `resolveAccentColors(colors, tone,
'subtle')` returns a **translucent** tint (`rgba(…, 0.13)` light, `0.24` dark).
Measuring a quiet text rung against that string reads the channels and ignores
the alpha, so the contrast you compute is not the contrast the reader gets. So
`shared.ts` flattens the tint over the surface the card actually landed on
(`useSurfaceFill()`), and every rung — title, excerpt, metadata — is measured
against the **composite**. `resolveNoteCardPaint` is pure, so the suite walks it
over both modes.

The same trap catches the TAG PILLS one component down, which is why
`noteTagVariant()` exists: the neutral tone's tint IS `contrast50`, the colour a
`tone="default"` card already painted, so a `subtle` pill on one is a label
floating on nothing at contrast 1.00. Those pills are drawn `outlined` instead —
the edge does what the fill cannot.

The same flattening does selection: the selected card is its own tone laid on
once more, which is why selection reads on a tinted card and on an untinted one
with one rule rather than two.

## What it owns, and what it does not

**Owns:** its geometry, its wash, which quiet rung each line sits at, the
composed accessible name, and the fact that a press in selection mode toggles.

**Does not own:** the layout of the board (give the cell a width — `grid` fills
it), the relative-time vocabulary (`meta.edited` is drawn as given), what a
notebook is, or whether a locked note's excerpt should be hidden. That last one
is the app's: `meta.locked` draws the marker and hides nothing, because Bloom
cannot know whether the excerpt it was handed is already redacted.

**The notebook TREE is not here either.** It already exists as `Sidebar`'s `tree`
prop and `SidebarFolder`; a second one would be a second answer.

## Traps

- **A 2px selected border must not move the content.** It does not: the padding
  gives the pixel back. If you restyle the card, keep that — a border that grows
  the box reflows the whole board on selection.
- **`tone` and `selected` compose.** A selected `error` note keeps its own edge
  colour and deepens by one surface step rather than by the tone laid twice —
  laying an OPAQUE tint over itself is a no-op, which is exactly what `default`
  did. Check both in dark mode if you add a tone.
- **The card publishes its surface.** Anything you put inside it through a
  slot will step off the card's real fill, tint included, because the card mounts
  `SurfaceLevelProvider` with the composited colour.
- **`loading` renders `NoteCardSkeleton`**, which is also exported on its own —
  a list that knows it is fetching ten notes should render ten skeletons rather
  than construct ten empty note objects to satisfy a required `title`.
