# NavigationBanner

`@oxy.so/bloom/navigation-banner`

`directions` is the route BEFORE you set off: the alternatives, the summary, the
step list. This is the route while you are on it — one maneuver at a time, over
a map, at arm's length.

```tsx
import {
  ArrivalBar,
  LaneGuidance,
  NavigationBanner,
  SpeedLimitPill,
} from '@oxy.so/bloom/navigation-banner';

<NavigationBanner
  maneuver="right"
  distance="400 m"
  instruction="Carrer del Roure"
  thenManeuver="left"
  then="onto Passatge de l'Om"
>
  <LaneGuidance lanes={lanes} />
</NavigationBanner>
```

## It reuses `directions`' maneuvers, it does not restate them

`maneuver` is `DirectionsManeuver` and the glyph comes from
`DIRECTIONS_MANEUVER_ICON`. A banner with its own arrow set would be a second
opinion about what "slight right" looks like, drawn next to a step list that
already had one — and the two would drift at the first icon change. The
announcement uses `DIRECTIONS_MANEUVER_LABELS` for the same reason.

`LaneDirection` is a SUBSET of the same vocabulary: a lane arrow is a maneuver
drawn small, with the members that make no sense painted on tarmac (`depart`,
`arrive`, `board`, …) left out.

## The pane is a `GlassIsland`, at a surface radius

Guidance is drawn over tiles Bloom does not own, and the island is the one
material in the library priced for that — a translucent pane that takes its fill
from a surface Bloom DOES own, so the text on it is legible whether the map
underneath is a night satellite tile or a white street map.

It is asked for `radius: 16` rather than its default capsule. A banner is a
surface; the pill rung belongs to button-like controls.

The island also mounts the `ControlSurface` the controls inside it read, which
is why nothing here writes `variant="glass"` on anything.

## The row

| part | rung |
| --- | --- |
| tile | 56 square, 12 radius, the state's tone as a tint, the glyph at 40 in that tone's accent |
| headline | `title-2-semibold`, TABULAR — the distance |
| instruction | `headline-medium`, up to two lines — the street |
| then | `body-2-medium` behind a full-bleed rule |
| children | behind a second rule |

The distance is the figure and the street is under it, because a driver reads
"how far" first and "onto what" second. Without a distance the street is
promoted to the figure rung rather than sitting small under an empty line —
which is what an arrival banner ("Arriving at Plaça de les Bruixes") looks like.

Tabular numerals matter more here than anywhere else in Bloom: the distance
counts down continuously, and proportional digits make the street name under it
shuffle sideways several times a second.

## `off-route` and `rerouting` are this component

Not a second surface. They take the same strip, in the same place, at the same
moment — a different pane appearing where this one was is a jump, and the driver
has to re-find the thing they were already reading.

What the state changes:

| state | glyph | tone | headline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `guiding` | the maneuver | `primary` | the distance |
| `off-route` | a warning | `error` | "Off route" |
| `rerouting` | a loop | `warning` | "Finding a new route" |

`rerouting` is deliberately NOT the error tone. Nothing is wrong yet, the app is
working, and a red banner at that moment tells a driver to act when the only
correct action is to keep driving.

In both states the maneuver stops being announced. A reader told to turn right
while the car is off the route has been told the one thing that is no longer
true.

## It is one utterance

The glyph is the only thing on screen that says which way to turn, and a glyph
says nothing aloud. So the maneuver row is a single `img` named by
`describeNavigationBanner` — "400 m, Turn right, Carrer del Roure, then turn
left onto Passatge de l'Om" — with its drawn text hidden from assistive
technology so the distance is not read twice.

`children` sit OUTSIDE that name. A lane row and a speed sign each announce
themselves, and folding them into the banner's sentence would make one
four-clause utterance that changes every few seconds.

## LaneGuidance

Lanes left to right as the driver sees them, numbered from 1 in the
announcement, because that is how you count them through a windscreen.

- A lane you may use takes the accent's tint; one you may not takes no fill at
  all. Two levels, not three: the question is binary.
- `preferred` picks the one arrow to take from an allowed lane and leaves its
  siblings at the quiet rung — a lane that also goes straight on is still the
  lane you want.
- A quiet arrow sits at the GRAPHICAL contrast rung (3:1), not a text one. It is
  a glyph, and flooring it at text contrast would make the lanes shout over the
  maneuver above them.
- A lane with several arrows grows sideways rather than stacking them. A cell
  taller than its neighbours breaks the count.

The row announces "Lane guidance, 4 lanes, use lane 3 and lane 4", and still
announces the count when none of them is yours.

## SpeedLimitPill

A 48 circle with a 4 ring, on the same chrome surface every floating map piece
paints, with `shadow-s`. It is a CIRCLE because it is a sign, not a capsule of
text.

`exceeded` FILLS the sign with its tone and takes that fill's own on-colour for
the number. A sign that merely got brighter is a sign a driver reads as the same
sign; the change has to survive a glance, in daylight, at an angle. Nothing
animates: a warning that flashes in a car asks for attention the road needs.

`tone` defaults to `error` and exists because not every jurisdiction posts a red
ring.

## ArrivalBar

The bottom strip: when you get there, how long is left, how far is left, and the
way out.

Three readings of EQUAL weight, each `title-3-semibold` and tabular over its own
caption. Peers because the driver is asking a different question of each one and
none of them is the answer to the other two.

The ending action is `Button` in `destructive` **at the 44 rung** — larger than
looks right in a screenshot, because it is pressed by a thumb, in a moving car,
at the bottom of the screen. It keeps its own chrome inside the island on
purpose: a control painted flush with the pane would be the quietest thing on a
strip whose only action stops the navigation.

### Why this is not `OrderStatusBar`

They look alike and they answer differently shaped questions, and the shape is
the whole component.

`OrderStatusBar` has ONE headline with an arrival reading beside it, a glyph
tile, a quiet second line and a progress meter. It says "here is what is
happening, and here is how far along it is" — it is for a journey you are
WAITING for. `ArrivalBar` has three readings of equal weight, no state and no
meter: the journey is happening, you are on it, and you pick whichever of the
three you wanted.

Giving `OrderStatusBar` a three-figure mode would be a second layout behind a
prop; giving `ArrivalBar` a status line and a meter would be a tracker for a
trip the user is driving.

What they do share is written down rather than duplicated: the same surface
rung, the same tabular figures, and the same refusal to compute anything —
neither reads a clock, formats a duration or knows what a kilometre is.

## Nothing here computes

Every string is pre-formatted by the app: the distance, the ETA, the remaining
time, the limit. There is no clock, no unit conversion and no route state
machine in this family.
